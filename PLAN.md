# RouteMaker: build specification

## What this is

RouteMaker is a web application for planning bicycle routes collaboratively, targeted at the Washington, DC metro area. It exists to solve two problems that existing tools such as Ride with GPS and Komoot handle poorly: planning a route with other people, and planning for different cycling modalities, where a route that is good for a solo rider is wrong for a dozen people and unusable for a thousand.

Users sign in with Discord, build or import routes, choose a modality that shapes how the router weighs roads, trails, hills, and turns, and put routes up for peer review. Reviewers comment on specific places, report issues and closures, record approvals, and propose alternatives that the owner can merge. Routes export as GPX, FIT, TCX, cue sheets, and marshal documents, and push directly to cycling services where an API exists.

Reader's note on precedence: decisions recorded as "owner decision" in this document were made by the project owner and are not open to reinterpretation by an implementing engineer.

## Region and data

- **Coverage.** The DC metro, clipped to a polygon covering roughly Frederick and Leesburg to the northwest, Annapolis to the east, and Fredericksburg to the south. Geofabrik extracts for DC, Maryland, and Virginia, merged then clipped with `osmium extract -s smart -S types=any` so boundary relations stay complete. Admin data is built with `valhalla_build_admins` from the merged extract before clipping, and `valhalla_build_timezones` is run so `date_time` requests work.
- **Elevation.** USGS 3DEP. Valhalla's elevation reader accepts only 1 arc-second, 3601 by 3601, big-endian int16 HGT tiles named like `N38W077.hgt`. Resample 3DEP with `gdalwarp` to that grid and write with GDAL's SRTMHGT driver; trim the 6-pixel overlap on 1 arc-second source tiles. The AWS terrain-tiles skadi set, which is 3DEP-derived for the US, is an acceptable shortcut. Elevation tiles are built once and cached on the tiles volume; the weekly rebuild reuses them. Elevation is re-pulled from 3DEP annually or when USGS publishes new coverage for the region; that rebuild is manual.
- **Base map.** A PMTiles file extracted with `pmtiles extract` from the Protomaps daily build against the coverage polygon, served by Caddy with HTTP range requests and read by MapLibre through the pmtiles protocol adapter. Glyphs and sprites are self-hosted. Caddy serves PMTiles, glyphs, and sprites only to requests whose `Origin` or `Referer` matches the app host, with a per-IP range-request limit. Refreshed monthly by the worker. Protomaps basemap layers do not carry surface or cycleway detail, so stress and infrastructure are drawn as an overlay from the application's own segment table. Do not use the public OpenStreetMap tile servers.
- **Stress data.** Stress is derived from OSM tags using the Furth 2017 Level of Traffic Stress tables as implemented by OSM-based classifiers such as BikeOttawa's, reimplemented rather than copied unless that code's license is recorded and compatible. Inputs: `highway`, `lanes`, `maxspeed`, `cycleway*`, `parking:*`, `oneway`, `surface`, with documented default assumptions where `lanes` or `maxspeed` is missing. DDOT bike lane data from Open Data DC (CC0) is conflated onto OSM ways by buffer overlap with documented precedence. Montgomery County's Level of Traffic Stress layer is subject to the license gate below.
- **Jurisdiction polygons.** Police jurisdiction, right-of-way owner, and park or trail manager are separate layers. Sources: DC boundary and the Architect of the Capitol jurisdiction polygon (`AOCPly`, the area the Capitol Police polices) from Open Data DC (OCTO, CC0); all NPS unit boundaries inside the coverage polygon from the NPS open data portal, in all three states, including President's Park for the White House complex; Census TIGER county layer (Virginia independent cities are county equivalents), TIGER place layer for municipalities with their own police such as Rockville, Gaithersburg, Takoma Park, Hyattsville, College Park, Laurel, Greenbelt, Bowie, Frederick, Annapolis, Vienna, Herndon, and Leesburg; TIGER military installations for Joint Base Anacostia-Bolling, Fort Myer, Fort Belvoir, and the Pentagon reservation; Arlington National Cemetery and the airport from the NPS and TIGER layers where present; M-NCPPC and NOVA Parks parcels from the county park data hubs; state road ownership from the Maryland SHA and VDOT road inventories. Federal land outside DC is first class: the GW Parkway and Mount Vernon Trail, Clara Barton Parkway, C&O Canal, BW Parkway, Suitland Parkway, and Greenbelt Park are all NPS.
- **Way-level override table.** A community-editable, admin-approved table of per-way corrections applied during preprocessing: jurisdiction overrides for DDOT arterials that cross NPS units such as Constitution and Independence Avenues through the Mall and Military Road and Massachusetts Avenue through Rock Creek Park, bridge legality corrections, and access corrections. Seeded from the verification fixtures in the Quality bar.
- **Refresh.** A weekly job pulls fresh extracts, runs preprocessing, rebuilds every Valhalla tile variant, validates them, and swaps tiles and the segment table together. See Operations for the swap procedure. The geocoder and basemap are refreshed monthly on their own path.
- **License gate.** Every non-federal source (Montgomery LTS, M-NCPPC parcels, NOVA Parks, Maryland SHA, VDOT, any reused classifier code) is ingested only after its license is recorded and found to permit display, redistribution in exports, and, where it feeds tile tags, ODbL-compatible derivative use. Sources that fail the check are replaced by OSM-derived or TIGER data. Open Data DC (CC0), NPS, USGS, and TIGER are public domain and compatible.
- **Licensing.** Every external source is documented with license, URL, and refresh procedure. The OSM extract with injected derived tags is an ODbL derivative database. To satisfy ODbL share-alike, the derived-tag database and the diff the pipeline produces are published under ODbL; the pipeline source code is published under an OSI license such as MIT or Apache-2.0. Contributions that feed preprocessing (issues, closures, override rows) are licensed by their authors under ODbL for data and CC BY-SA for text, stated in the sign-in terms and on the issue and override forms; info card text is CC BY-SA. Attribution "© OpenStreetMap contributors" appears on every map, thumbnail, cue sheet, export download page, and in GPX metadata, alongside "© Protomaps" on maps. USGS 3DEP, Census TIGER, Open Data DC, and NPS are credited on a data page.

## Architecture and stack

- **Frontend.** React with TypeScript and MapLibre GL. Viewing, commenting, and issue reporting work on phones; the editor is desktop-first and best effort on mobile. No native app and no offline mode.
- **Backend.** Python with FastAPI. Pydantic models for all API types. Async calls to Valhalla and external services. Alembic migrations run in a one-shot `migrate` compose service that the API and worker depend on with `condition: service_completed_successfully`.
- **Database.** PostGIS. Stores users, sessions, routes, route versions, a route audience join table of route and guild, control points, collaborators, segments with attributes, a crossings table for border control nodes, comments, issues, proposals, approvals, notifications, jurisdiction polygons, jurisdiction info cards, the override table, connected accounts, push logs, settings, and an audit log. All user-authored rows use `deleted_at` soft deletes purged after 30 days, including files on the uploads volume owned by purged rows. Each route version stores a frozen copy of the modality weights and jurisdiction settings used, not only a modality id.
- **Worker.** Procrastinate, which is Postgres-backed and asyncio-native, so no broker service is needed. Jobs are idempotent by explicit key such as `push:{route_version_id}:{service}`, retried with exponential backoff up to 5 times, with per-type timeouts (export 2 minutes, push 5 minutes, rebuild 6 hours). The rebuild and the backup are periodic tasks; the rebuild is single-flight. Failed jobs appear on an admin page and alert after the final retry. Nothing slow runs in the request path.
- **Routing engine.** Valhalla, self-hosted, bicycle costing, one process per tile variant.
- **Geocoder.** Photon, self-hosted, for place search in the editor. Photon indexes from a Nominatim database, not from a PBF, so it is populated either from GraphHopper's per-country Photon dump filtered to the coverage bounding box or from a one-off Nominatim import of the clipped extract, documented as the heavier option. It runs with a 3 GB memory limit and a per-user rate limit of 10 requests per second, and is not on the weekly rebuild path.
- **Thumbnail renderer.** A small Node sidecar using `@maplibre/maplibre-gl-native` against the local PMTiles and style, called by the worker over the compose network.
- **Deployment.** One Docker Compose stack on one AWS host: Caddy for TLS and static files, the frontend, the API, the worker, the migrate service, PostGIS, Photon, the thumbnail renderer, and one Valhalla container per active tile variant. `compose.yaml` plus `compose.override.yaml` for local development, secrets via `.env` with a committed `.env.example`, all images pinned, all services `restart: unless-stopped`. The same stack must run locally.
- **Preprocessing pipeline.** Python using osmium, Shapely, and rasterio. It is the core of the product and is built and tested first. It computes per-way derived values and writes them in three places:
  1. Into the PostGIS segment table, keyed by `(osm_way_id, ordinal within way)` plus stored geometry: stress tier, stress time windows, jurisdiction layers, bridge legality split into roadway and sidepath, lane count and width estimate, sinuosity and sharp bends per mile, lit, reviewer penalty, and elevation samples. The pipeline writes to a staging schema; see Operations for the swap.
  2. Into the OSM extract as tags that a project-owned copy of `graph.lua` (configured via `mjolnir.graph_lua_name`) remaps onto Valhalla's existing edge attributes. Valhalla's tile schema is fixed and unknown tags are dropped, so every derived value that must influence routing is expressed through an attribute the bicycle costing already reads. The mapping table is part of the repository and unit tested: stress tier drives `cycleway`, `highway` class, and `maxspeed` overrides; reviewer penalty downgrades `surface` or raises `maxspeed`; bridge legality sets `bicycle=yes/no` per roadway or sidepath way; `lit` is preserved. Jurisdiction is not stored in tiles. The remap is global and shared by every modality; there are no per-preset remaps.
  3. It splits every way where it crosses a state line and inserts a node tagged `barrier=border_control`, which Valhalla treats as a border control node. This makes the existing `country_crossing_cost` and `country_crossing_penalty` request options act as a per-request state crossing penalty with no engine fork. The Lua mapping is verified not to deny bicycle access at those nodes. Each inserted node's OSM id, coordinates, and the two states it separates are recorded in the crossings table, because Valhalla keeps no custom node tags; the application resolves crossing direction from that table.
- **Segment identity across rebuilds.** Comments, issues, closures, cue overrides, and reviewer penalties store a geometry anchor (snapped point or linestring) plus the segment key at the time of creation. After each swap a reconciliation job re-resolves anchors by way id and nearest geometry, flags unresolved anchors for review, and never silently drops them. Route versions store control points, waypoints, modality snapshot, and resulting geometry, never graph edge ids.
- **Stats source.** Per-edge attributes for a route come from `/trace_attributes` with `shape_match: edge_walk` on the route shape. Valhalla edges split at graph intersections, which do not coincide with pipeline segments, so the join uses `edge.way_id` plus linear overlap of the edge shape against stored segment geometry, apportioning length where an edge spans several segments. Turn and left-turn counts come from the maneuver list. Elevation gain and loss come from `elevation_interval` on `/route` with a hysteresis threshold so DEM noise does not inflate gain.

## Routing model

Valhalla's bicycle costing exposes a fixed set of dials. Every modality behavior is assigned to exactly one of four layers, and the code makes the assignment explicit:

1. **Tile build.** Derived tags remapped through `graph.lua`, border control nodes at state lines, and variant tile sets for behavior costing cannot express. Trail-class ways are defined once as `highway` in cycleway, footway, path, pedestrian, bridleway, or steps regardless of bicycle tag, plus sidepath-only bridge ways, because DC-area trails are tagged inconsistently. Variants: standard; mass ride, with trail-class ways removed; e-bike, with `electric_bicycle=no` ways set to `bicycle=no`. Each variant is its own Valhalla process. Variant behaviors are toggles, not sliders. The mass ride and e-bike variants are mutually exclusive until the phase 6 path-avoidance dial; the UI disables "E-bike rules" while bike paths are disallowed and explains why. If the exclusivity proves limiting, a combined mass ride e-bike variant is an additional tile build and Valhalla process, not a fork, and is preferred over advancing phase 6. A closed-course variant that relaxes `bicycle=no` on parkways and limited-access roadways for permitted events is a recorded follow-up after phase 6, not in scope for first release.
2. **Per-request costing options.** `bicycle_type`, `use_roads`, `use_hills`, `avoid_bad_surfaces`, `use_living_streets`, `maneuver_penalty`, `gate_cost`, `gate_penalty`, `country_crossing_cost`, `country_crossing_penalty`. Request-level parameters: `exclude_polygons` for small closure polygons only, `date_time`, `elevation_interval`. Requests use `date_time.type: 3` (invariant) with the planning time, which keeps bidirectional A* and alternates while still evaluating conditional restrictions; types 1 and 2 are used only for an explicit arrive-by request.
3. **A small costing fork**, deferred to phase 6 and kept as a patch set against upstream with a documented rebase procedure: apply the existing `use_lit` and `unlit_factor_` in bicycle costing, and add a path-avoidance dial so the mass ride variant becomes unnecessary. Before implementing, check whether the current release already provides either.
4. **App-level search and scoring.** Dragging a waypoint triggers a single-pass preview using the modality's layer 1 and 2 dials. Layer 4 search runs on release or on an explicit "Find alternatives" action, as a worker job with progress for Mass Ride and Mountain Goat, and presents the ranked candidates with their stats blocks so the owner chooses; the chosen candidate is what gets saved. Multi-pass routing and ranking of candidates on turn count, left turns across traffic, uncontrolled crossings of high-stress ways, road width, sinuosity, unpaved share, lit share, stress breakdown, crowded-trail time windows, and jurisdiction crossings. Candidate generation does not rely on Valhalla `alternates` for multi-waypoint routes, since alternates exist only in bidirectional A* and are disabled for more than two locations; candidates come from per-leg two-location alternates requests or from multi-pass requests with perturbed dials, and the perturbed-dial set always includes at least one pass at high `use_roads` so a road-based alternative is present for sinuosity ranking to choose. The left-turn-across-traffic classifier uses `oneway` on the source and target ways, so a left from a one-way onto a one-way is not counted while a left from a two-way is. Traffic circles are keyed on a curated list, not tags alone, since DC circles are tagged inconsistently.

A modality is a named weight vector over these layers, stored as data. Every dial exists on every preset, so "Mass Ride, Night" or "Group Ride, DC only" are savable customs, subject to the variant exclusivity above.

**User-facing controls.** Sliders in plain language: "Quiet roads to direct roads", "Avoid hills", "Fewer turns and stops", "Paved only to gravel OK", "Ride two abreast", "Stay in home state". Toggles: "Allow bike paths and trails", "Prefer lit streets", "E-bike rules". Mass Ride ships with "Allow bike paths and trails" off; Group Ride ships with it on. A collapsed Advanced panel shows the raw dial mapping. Mountain Goat and Bikepacking show hills, surface, distance budget, and "Stay in home state" by default; the remaining dials are in the Advanced panel.

Presets:

| Modality | Intent | Layer assignment |
|---|---|---|
| Default | Normal cycling priorities | L2: Hybrid, mid use_roads, mid use_hills |
| Trailmaxxing | Lowest stress ride, directness secondary | L2: use_roads near zero, living streets on, low surface avoidance. L4: stress-weighted candidate ranking, crowded-trail window penalty |
| Group Ride | About a dozen riders, quiet roads, no winding paths, room to ride two abreast | L1: standard variant, trails allowed; the "Allow bike paths and trails" toggle switches to the mass ride variant when off. L2: mid use_roads, high maneuver_penalty, high gate_cost. L4: winding-path penalty from per-segment sinuosity and sharp bends per mile, left-turn-across-traffic scoring, penalty for uncontrolled crossings of high-stress ways, preference for signalized arterial crossings, width scoring, suggested regroup points at the top of each climb over a configurable gain |
| Mass Ride | Hundreds to thousands of riders, two or more lanes, few turns, low grade tolerance, no bike paths | L1: mass ride variant (path-avoidance dial after phase 6), roadway bridge legality. L2: use_roads at max, use_hills near zero, high maneuver_penalty, country_crossing penalty high. L4: left-turn and uncontrolled-crossing scoring, lanes and width from tags with highway-class fallback and parking-lane reduction, maximum grade cap with descents over the cap flagged separately, rider count input driving a peloton length estimate that checks for self-crossing, doubling back, and out-and-back sections |

L4: gap warning listing any top-tier stress distance that could not be avoided; the router degrades to a penalty rather than returning no route |
| Fast | Few stops, few turns, smooth pavement | L2: high use_roads, high maneuver_penalty, high surface avoidance |
| Recovery | Minimize elevation | L2: use_hills zero |

| Bikepacking | Weight services and camping | L4: points-of-interest scoring, distance budget |

**State crossing penalty.** A per-modality property expressed through `country_crossing_cost` and `country_crossing_penalty` at the border control nodes. Mass Ride default high. Every other modality default off, where off means the request explicitly sends both options as zero: Valhalla's default cost is 600 seconds and would otherwise apply at every border control node. Both are capped at 43200 by Valhalla. Adjustable by the "Stay in home state" slider on every preset from day one. The penalty is symmetric and charges on leaving and on returning.

**Home jurisdiction.** The state of the start control point, or of the first waypoint if no start control point exists, DC, Maryland, or Virginia, overridable per route. Direction of a crossing is resolved from the crossings table.

**Stats block.** Every route returns: distance, elevation gain and loss, grade profile with maximum grade and flagged steep descents, surface breakdown, turn count, left turns across traffic, intersection summary by control type, stress breakdown by distance, unpaved share, lit share, sinuosity, jurisdiction breakdown by distance for each layer, crossing data, gap warnings, and freehand distance. The stats block always carries crossing data; the route summary surfaces it as its own section only when jurisdiction warnings are on and otherwise folds it into the breakdown.

**Time-dependent behavior.** `date_time` affects only conditional access restrictions and time zones in Valhalla, so bicycle routing does not change by time of day for roads that are always bike-legal. When no planning time is set, requests assume the next Saturday at 9:00 local time, shown in the editor as an assumption the owner can change, so that conditional restrictions are evaluated rather than ignored. Time-dependent stress, such as the lower Beach Drive and Sligo Creek Parkway weekend car closures, is stored on the segment table as a default value plus windowed values and applied in stats and layer 4 scoring using the route's planning time. Valhalla does not read `oneway:conditional`, so the Rock Creek and Potomac Parkway weekday rush-hour reversal is expressed by preprocessing as directional conditional access restrictions (`bicycle:forward:conditional` and `bicycle:backward:conditional`), verified against the parser's supported keys in a unit test. Reviewer-confirmed closures with date ranges are injected as `access:conditional` at the next rebuild using month-day time domains, since Valhalla time domains carry no year; the rebuild removes them once the end date has passed. While a closure is active before its first rebuild it is supplied as a small `exclude_polygons` entry.

## Jurisdiction model

Jurisdiction is a set of per-segment attributes, not a single value:

- **Police jurisdiction.** DC under MPD; federal units by agency (US Park Police by NPS unit, Capitol Police, Secret Service Uniformed Division for President's Park, military police for installations, MWAA for the airport); M-NCPPC park land mapped to the relevant Park Police division, Montgomery or Prince George's, the same way NPS units map to US Park Police; Maryland municipality or county; Virginia town, city, or county.
- **Right-of-way owner.** DDOT, NPS, Maryland SHA, VDOT, county, municipality, or park authority.
- **Park or trail manager** where different, such as NOVA Parks for the W&OD and the Fairfax County Park Authority.

The crossing list and the count of "distinct authorities with jurisdiction" use the police layer. The owner and manager layers appear in the breakdown.

**Crossing detection rules.** Crossings shorter than a configurable minimum, default 0.1 mile, are collapsed unless a control point lies inside them, which handles the Jones Point sliver on the Wilson Bridge path and the circles that straddle the DC line. Ways with `bridge=yes` or `tunnel=yes` and nonzero `layer` over a park polygon inherit the adjacent jurisdiction, defined as the jurisdiction of the connected non-bridge ways at each end; if the two ends differ, the bridge is split at its midpoint. Segments on Western, Eastern, and Southern Avenues are treated as DC with a boundary flag listing both authorities; this rule is not surfaced in UI copy as a legal statement. The override table handles DDOT arterials through NPS units.

**Per-route settings.** Two separately editable settings, each stored with an "explicitly set" flag. Changing modality recomputes defaults only for settings the user has not explicitly set, and the UI shows which values are defaults and which are choices. Modality controls routing only.

- **Ride purpose.** Recreational, First Amendment assembly, or special event, each with a one-line plain-language explanation shown when the control is surfaced: a ride for its own sake; a protest, demonstration, or expressive event; a permitted race, charity ride, or festival. It selects which info card copy is shown and appears in the route summary next to the jurisdiction breakdown. The default purpose for Mass Ride is a per-server setting chosen by the server admin. Owner decision: the shipped default is First Amendment assembly. Every other modality defaults to recreational.
- **Jurisdiction warnings.** On or off. Default on when the modality is Mass Ride or the purpose is First Amendment assembly or special event; off only for another modality with recreational purpose.

| Modality | Purpose | Warnings default |
|---|---|---|
| Mass Ride | Any | On |
| Any other | First Amendment assembly or special event | On |
| Any other | Recreational | Off |

**When warnings are on:**

- A state line crossing is a strong warning. It fires when a waypoint or control point is placed outside the home jurisdiction, not only after routing. The state line warning supersedes the per-authority notice for the same crossing.
- Entering a federal enclave or other non-home police jurisdiction produces a notice at a per-authority level set by the server admin in that authority's info card: note or warning. Owner decision: the shipped default for every federal enclave inside DC is note, drawn as a map overlay and counted in the breakdown, because in practice passing through is rarely an issue. Admins may raise Capitol grounds or President's Park to warning for their server.
- A control point placed inside a federal enclave gets a note regardless of the pass-through level, because stopping is where problems arise.
- The route summary lists crossings in order with mile markers, miles per jurisdiction, and the count of distinct authorities. A state line crossing where the police authority is unchanged, such as Memorial Bridge onto the GW Parkway, carries the note "authority unchanged: US Park Police". Crossings can optionally appear in cue sheets as "Entering Montgomery County, MD" lines.

**When warnings are off:** no warnings, no notes. The overlay is available as a toggle in every modality.

**Info cards.** Each authority has a community-maintained card: contact, typical lead time, links, notes, and the per-server notice level. Reviewer and above can edit; edits are versioned with author and timestamp and revertable by admins; each card shows "last edited" and the line "community-maintained notes, not legal advice, verify with the agency". The application states facts about jurisdictions and never makes legal claims in code.

## Route model

- **Structure.** A route has a name, description, tags, a modality snapshot, waypoints that shape the path, and control points with a type: start, finish, rest, regroup, marshal post, SAG or medical, sweep, bail-out. Control points are distinct from waypoints. A route has an owning guild, chosen by the owner at creation from the guilds they are a member of and defaulting to their only guild, and an audience, a set of guilds that always includes the owning guild. Reviewers and server visibility, the routes index, and the Needs review queue resolve against the audience, so a ride co-organized by several clubs is reviewed by all of them. Per-server settings, the default purpose, and the per-authority notice levels come from the owning guild alone so they are never ambiguous. A single-guild deployment sees no difference. A route has an optional planning date and time, a planning speed, and an optional expected rider count, used only to set `date_time` on routing requests, compute ETAs on cues, drive time-window scoring, and feed the peloton length estimate and marshal ETAs. This is not an event.
- **Visibility.** Private (owner and named collaborators), reviewers (owner, collaborators, and users with the reviewer role), server (all signed-in members of any audience guild), link (anyone with the link, unlisted), or public (listed and readable by anyone). Default private. Unpermitted rides commonly keep the route secret until ride day, so nothing about a route is broadcast without an explicit owner action. Link visibility exists because committees routinely need one outside person, an agency liaison or a club officer who is not on the Discord, to see a route; it renders a read-only interactive map, stats, and the rider cue sheet, with no comments, and downloads chosen by the owner from the rider cue sheet PDF (default on), the overview map PDF, and the intersection list PDF. Everything served to an unauthenticated link viewer comes from data stored per version: geometry, stats, compiled cues, and PDFs rendered by the worker when the version is saved; unauthenticated requests never trigger routing, geocoding, thumbnail rendering, or export jobs. Whether link visibility requires login is a server setting `link_requires_login`, editable on the admin settings page; when true the tier is labeled "members with link" and admits members of any audience guild and the unauthenticated surface reduces to the generic OpenGraph card. Owner decision: the shipped default is false. Both modes are covered by the visibility tests. Visibility governs who can open a route in every state; draft additionally excludes the route from the index, the queue, and Discord posting, so a draft at link visibility is reachable only through its link.
- **Public visibility.** Public is link visibility made discoverable and commentable. The route appears in a public index, is served to anyone without login, and is the only tier crawlers may index; every other tier stays `noindex`. Public routes get a stable slug URL alongside the token URL. The owner chooses downloads from the same list as link, extended with the GPX track, since a published community ride wants riders to take the route with them. Reading stays unauthenticated and pre-rendered on the same terms as link: no routing, geocoding, rendering, or export jobs on an anonymous request. Writing requires a Discord login. A signed-in user who belongs to no audience guild has guest standing on a public route: they can comment and open issues, and nothing else. Whether guests may write at all is a server setting `public_guest_comments`, default on, and an owner can turn it off for one route; with it off, public routes are read-only to non-members and the comment box explains why. Guest content is rate-limited more tightly than member content, is marked as from outside the organizing guilds wherever it appears, and cannot be confirmed into routing penalties by its author, since confirming remains a reviewer action. Choosing public asks the owner to confirm once, because an unpermitted ride's route is exactly what this tier publishes. Setting public on a draft is allowed and behaves as link until the route leaves draft, since draft already withholds a route from every listing.
- **Named collaborators.** Added and removed by the owner or an admin, and notified when added. A collaborator can view the route in any state, comment, open issues, and submit proposals against it. An owner-granted per-collaborator `edit` flag, recorded in the audit log, allows saving new versions, because committees often have two people drafting one route. Merge, reject, state changes, and Discord posting remain owner only. A viewer-role member added as a collaborator gains those abilities on that route only.
- **Share links.** A per-route 128-bit random token, revocable and regenerable by the owner. Unauthenticated OpenGraph metadata (name, distance, elevation) and the static PNG thumbnail are served only for link visibility, since Discord fetches OpenGraph data whenever a link is pasted and for a secret route the thumbnail is the secret. At every other visibility, unauthenticated requests receive a generic card with the name only and no route data, and the thumbnail is served only to logged-in users the visibility admits. Thumbnails are rendered by the worker when a version is saved on a route at link visibility or in review or published, cached per version. Share pages send `Referrer-Policy: same-origin`, and `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` on every tier except public; Caddy and API access logs redact the token segment of share-link paths.
- **Versions.** Every save creates a version with a history view. Published and in-review routes are never re-routed automatically; the owner may still save new versions. Geometry and compiled cues are stored per version, and re-routing happens only on an explicit owner action. Active closures and weekly rebuilds produce alerts on affected routes, never silent re-routes. The rebuild emits a drift report listing routes whose segments changed attributes or lost their edges.
- **States.** Draft, in review, published, archived. A draft at reviewers visibility is openable by reviewers but accepts comments and proposals only from named collaborators. Requesting review pins a version and requires visibility of at least reviewers; if the route is private, the request dialog prompts the owner to raise visibility first. Archived is read-only and hidden from lists; it accepts no new comments or proposals, existing threads stay readable, and share links still resolve subject to visibility.
- **Proposals.** A proposal replaces the sub-range between two points shared with the base version and is merged by splicing, which matches how committees actually work: "take your detour around Thomas Circle, keep the rest". A whole-route proposal is the degenerate case. Proposals carry `base_version_id` and a state: open, merged, rejected, withdrawn, or superseded, where superseded means the proposer pushed a rebased proposal against a newer base, closing the earlier one and linking to the replacement. A proposer can push new versions to an open proposal until it closes. Merging creates a new head version whose provenance records the proposal and author. If the base has advanced past the proposal's base, the compare view shows base, current head, and proposal, and the owner either asks the proposer to rebase or splices manually. A rejected proposal is retained and readable and can be reopened as a new proposal via fork.
- **Approvals.** Reviewers record approve or request changes against the pinned version. The owner can re-pin the current version from the review panel; existing approvals remain attached to the version they were recorded on, and the summary shows "approved on version N, current version M" and whether the current version differs from the last approved one.
- **Forking.** Any contributor or above can fork a route they can see.
- **Editor.** Draggable waypoints, insert and delete waypoints mid-route, snapped and freehand segments, reverse, close loop, out-and-back, split the route into two routes at a point, undo and redo, place search via Photon, an elevation profile with hover synced to the map, cue override editing, visibility and collaborator management, planning time, speed, and rider count inputs, delete route, and the share link.
- **Closures.** A closure is an issue with a date range and a polygon or segment set. Confirmed closures feed the router as described in Routing model and produce alerts on routes that cross them. The closures table is seeded from a checked-in list of known long-term closures in the coverage area, verified at build time.

**Permissions.** The first admin is set by Discord user id in the environment file. Admins can ban a Discord user id regardless of guild roles.

| View routes visible to them, comments, info cards | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Create and edit own routes, change own route state | | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Comment, open issues, submit proposals, fork | | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Confirm and resolve issues and closures | | | Yes | Yes |
| Record approve or request changes | | | Yes | Yes |
| Edit info cards and propose override table rows | | | Yes | Yes |
| Hide comments and issues | | | Yes | Yes |

Guest standing, a signed-in user with no audience guild membership, applies only to public routes with `public_guest_comments` on: comment and open issues, nothing else. Guests cannot fork, propose, approve, confirm, or see any other tier.
| Approve override table rows, delete any route, ban users, edit role mapping, revert cards, edit server settings | | | | Yes |

Owner-scoped actions, available to the route owner and to admins: edit the route and save versions (also collaborators with the `edit` flag), change state, re-pin, merge or reject proposals, add or remove collaborators, add or remove audience guilds (only guilds the owner belongs to, recorded in the audit log), transfer ownership to a named collaborator (recorded in the audit log), set visibility, choose link-visibility downloads, regenerate the share link, post to Discord. Adding an audience guild is a sharing action and the main way a secret route leaks, so the audience is shown on the route summary next to the visibility tier. Admins of the owning guild moderate the route; an admin of any other audience guild can remove their own guild from the audience, which is their only lever over someone else's route.

Comments, issues, and proposals are open on in-review and published routes. Draft routes accept comments and proposals only from named collaborators. Confirming an issue records reviewer id and timestamp in the audit log.

**Comments.** Belong to a route, record the version they were made on, and carry a map anchor (point, or way id plus offset, with geometry). On a later version, a comment is shown at its anchor if the segment is still on the route and listed as "from an earlier version" otherwise. Comments are never deleted by geometry changes. Comments on a proposal form the proposal thread; a merged proposal's thread is linked from the resulting version's history, and a rejected proposal's thread stays readable. Comments and issue descriptions are capped at 5,000 characters.

**Issues.** Attached to map segments or polygons, not to a route. Categories: safety, surface, closure, navigation, other. Lifecycle: open, confirmed, resolved, won't fix. Any route whose geometry touches an open or confirmed issue shows it, with the reporting route recorded as context. Confirmed safety and surface issues feed the reviewer penalty tag at the next rebuild.

**Discovery.** A routes index with list and map views filtered by state, modality, owner, distance range, start-area bounding box, and name text, showing only routes the viewer's visibility admits. A "My routes" view. A public index of public routes, reachable without login and the only listing crawlers see. A "Needs review" queue of in-review routes and open proposals sortable by age with counts of open comments and issues, showing the routes of every guild the viewer belongs to, with a per-guild filter. If several organizations repeatedly co-plan, an admin may define a named coalition, a saved set of guilds an owner can add to the audience in one action; this is convenience over the audience list and is not required for first release.

**Notifications.** In-app notification list. Events: proposal submitted (owner), proposal merged or rejected (proposer), comment or reply on a thread (owner and participants), guest comment or issue on a public route (owner), issue confirmed or resolved (reporter), approve or request changes recorded (owner), collaborator saved a new version (owner), added as collaborator (collaborator), ownership transferred (new owner), route crosses a newly confirmed closure (owner and collaborators with the edit flag), drift report affects a route (owner and collaborators with the edit flag). Discord DMs are out of scope.

**Discord channel posting.** An explicit per-route action by the owner, never automatic, honoring visibility. Webhook posts set `allowed_mentions` to none, truncate user text to 500 characters, and are rate-limited to one message per route per 10 minutes.

**Moderation and abuse limits.** GPX uploads: max 20 MB, max 100,000 track points, parsed with `defusedxml`, rejected if not well-formed GPX 1.0 or 1.1. Per-user rate limits enforced in the API: routing 60 per minute, import 10 per hour, export 30 per hour, comments and issues 30 per hour, and for guests on public routes 5 per hour with a total cap per route per day. A Discord account younger than a configurable age cannot post as a guest. Admins can hide guest content, ban a Discord user id, and disable guest writing server-wide; every such action is audited. All unauthenticated paths, including the public index, OpenGraph, thumbnails, the read-only view, stats, cue sheet, and downloads, are limited at Caddy to 60 requests per minute per IP, with file downloads limited to 10 per minute per IP. Mountain Goat and multi-pass searches cap candidates at a configured number. An `audit_log` table records actor, action, target, and before and after for issue lifecycle changes, closure confirmation, info card edits, override approvals, proposal merge and reject, approvals, collaborator changes, audience changes, ownership transfers, hide actions, role-mapping changes, and bans.

**Review is asynchronous.** Live collaborative editing is out of scope.

## Import and export

- **Import.** GPX tracks and routes. Tracks are downsampled or chunked to stay inside Valhalla's `trace.max_shape` and `trace.max_distance` limits, or the limits are raised in config. Run `trace_attributes` first to classify points as matched, interpolated, or unmatched; split at unmatched runs; `trace_route` each matched run; unmatched runs become freehand segments flagged in the UI. GPX routes with at most 25 route points (configurable, well inside Valhalla's default bicycle `max_locations` of 50) are routed through `/route` with each point as a `through` location, falling back to the track path if a point snaps to a `bicycle=no` way and the request fails; denser routes, which is how Ride with GPS, Komoot, and Garmin export them, are treated as tracks and map-matched. Imported waypoints are kept as generic cue overrides at their position along the route, or as control points when their name matches start, finish, or rest patterns, and the user can promote or discard them. The turn model emits an "Enter unmapped section, continue N mi" cue at the start of a freehand run and a normal cue at re-entry; freehand distance counts toward mile markers.
- **Compiled artifact.** Every export starts from one compiled route: Valhalla directions plus a per-route cue override layer where the owner can add a cue at a location, edit cue text, suppress a cue, or merge adjacent cues, so cues like "CAUTION rail tracks, cross at right angle", "single file on bridge", and "regroup at top" are first class. Overrides are anchored by a snapped geometry point, not by index or distance; distance-along is recomputed at compile time, so reverse, split, and out-and-back preserve them, and the compiler reports overrides whose anchor no longer lies on the route. Cue types: left, right, slight and sharp variants, straight, u-turn, roundabout exit with exit number and cross street, summit, valley, water, food, danger, generic, and jurisdiction. Each format has a documented mapping from these types. Bear-versus-turn thresholds are tuned against the reference routes because the L'Enfant diagonals produce acute intersections.
- **Formats.** GPX track with waypoints for cues and attribution in metadata; FIT course with course points pinned to the nearest record; TCX; rider cue sheet as HTML and PDF; handlebar-format narrow cue sheet; marshal cue sheet with control points, regroup holds, and ETAs; intersection list as CSV and PDF; printable overview map PDF with numbered turns. The intersection list covers every intersection with cross traffic, with mile marker, control type distinguishing signal, all-way stop, route stops, cross traffic stops, uncontrolled, and circle entry from OSM stop and direction tags, marked "unknown, verify" where tags are missing, turn direction, and blank columns for marshal assignments; alleys and driveways are excluded by default with a toggle. For Mass Ride, the marshal cue sheet and intersection list print front and rear ETAs computed from planning speed and the peloton length estimate. Exports accept a max-cue budget per device profile and drop lowest-priority cues first, since older head units truncate beyond roughly 200 course points; the budget applies to GPX waypoints as well as FIT and TCX course points, because Garmin and Hammerhead convert GPX waypoints to course points on import. Owner-added cues and danger-type cues are never dropped. FIT files are written with the Garmin FIT Python SDK under the FIT Protocol License, or fit-tool under BSD-3; the choice and license are recorded in the data-source document.
- **Delivery tiers.** Direct push where an API exists, manual upload to a hub the device syncs from, and download with per-device instructions. Every route always has the download path.
- **Integrations, as verified at the time of writing and to be re-verified before implementation.** Direct push: Wahoo (Cloud API, `POST /v1/routes` with a FIT file, `routes_write` scope, sandbox then production review; pushes set `external_id` to the route id and `provider_updated_at` to the version timestamp so re-pushes update in place) and Hammerhead (Developer Platform public API, self-serve developer account and API license, supports webhooks). Garmin's Connect Developer Program has paused new partner applications; the Courses API is implemented only if the program reopens and is not scheduled. Ride with GPS's supported v1 API does not create routes, and Strava's API is read and export only for routes, so Ride with GPS, Strava, Komoot, and COROS are manual-upload hubs with per-service instructions. COROS and Komoot partner APIs are not attainable for a community app.
- **Connected accounts.** Per-user OAuth tokens per service, encrypted in the application layer as described in Operations. Each account records provider, provider user id, scopes, expiry, last refresh, and status: ok, reconnect required, revoked. Refresh is serialized per account with a row lock; a failed refresh sets reconnect required and stops pushes rather than retrying forever. A provider deauthorization webhook or a 401 on refresh sets status revoked and stops pushes. Each push records the provider route id and the version pushed, so a new version updates the remote route rather than duplicating it; archiving a route does not delete it remotely. Disconnect calls the provider revoke endpoint where one exists. Push logs are visible to the user and retained 90 days.

## Auth and community

- **Discord OAuth only.** Scopes `identify guilds guilds.members.read`. Guild membership and role ids are recorded per configured guild at login. Application permission is the maximum across configured guilds of the mapped roles, via a config table `guild_id, role_id, permission`. A user's permission on a given route is the maximum across only those audience guilds they belong to, so a reviewer in one club does not gain reviewer powers on another club's routes. Discord access and refresh tokens are stored as a connected account with the same encryption as partner tokens and refreshed by the worker, since Discord access tokens expire in 7 days. The Discord row is internal: it does not appear in the connected-services list and has no push log. A key-encryption key load failure is a fatal startup error for API and worker, not a token error; the sweep treats a decryption failure as transient and never logs users out for it.
- **Re-validation.** Membership and roles are re-checked against Discord when a request arrives more than an hour after the last check, performed with a 2-second timeout, and by a six-hourly worker sweep. On a Discord 5xx or timeout the cached permission is kept and the sweep retries. Only a 401 or 404 or a confirmed loss of membership revokes all of that user's sessions and downgrades access to none. If the Discord refresh token is invalid the user is logged out and must sign in again; content is unaffected.
- **Sessions.** Server-side rows in Postgres referenced by an HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax cookie; 30 days idle, 90 days absolute. State-changing requests require a CSRF token.
- **Account deletion.** Revokes all sessions immediately, revokes tokens at the provider where an endpoint exists, deletes connected-account and push-log rows, assigns authored comments, issues, and proposals to an anonymous sentinel user, and retains only a hashed Discord id tombstone for ban enforcement. Owned routes with named collaborators or recorded approvals are transferred to the first collaborator with the edit flag; if none exists, the owning guild's admins are notified to reassign within 30 days before purge, because route leads change every season. Owned routes with no collaborators and no approvals at private or reviewers visibility are soft-deleted and purged after 30 days, since no remaining viewer could fork them and an unpermitted ride's route should not outlive its owner; such routes at server, link, or public visibility are archived and remain forkable. Approvals, info card edits, and override rows keep their audit rows. Audit log rows keep the numeric actor id and display "deleted user".

## Operations

- **Secrets and tokens.** Per-user OAuth tokens are encrypted in the application layer with AES-256-GCM, never in SQL. The key-encryption key is stored as an SSM SecureString, which is KMS-backed, and read once at process start; locally it comes from the environment file. It is never written to the database and is not in dumps. Each ciphertext row carries a `key_id`; rotation adds a new id, re-encrypts rows in a worker job, and retires the old id. Discord client secret, webhook URLs, and partner app credentials get the same handling. S3 access uses the instance IAM role.
- **Backups.** A Procrastinate periodic task runs `pg_dump -Fc` through a sidecar with the matching client version, uploads to S3 with SSE-KMS and 30-day retention, verifies the upload with `pg_restore --list`, and writes a success row that the alert reads. A daily EBS snapshot of the data volume, retained 14 days, covers uploaded GPX originals, exported PDFs, and thumbnails. The `.env` file and key-encryption key are stored in SSM so a host rebuild can recover them. Tiles and graphs are rebuildable and not backed up. Restore runbook: provision host, pull compose, restore secrets from SSM, `pg_restore` the latest dump, run migrations, rebuild tiles from cached extracts, run the reference-route tests as a smoke check. Target RPO 24 hours, RTO 4 hours. A restore drill runs before first public release and quarterly.
- **Rebuild and swap.** Valhalla does not reload tiles at runtime. The rebuild writes tiles to a dated directory and packs a tile extract per variant, and writes the new segment table and derived attributes to a staging schema. It starts a second Valhalla container per variant against the new extracts and runs the reference-route tests against them. On pass, the swap is one step: rename staging to live inside a single transaction, then repoint the API upstream per variant through a settings table the API watches, reloading its HTTP client without a restart, and stop the old containers. On fail, drop staging, keep the old tiles, and alert. The previous extracts are retained for rollback. The reconciliation job and drift report run after the swap against the live table. Disk is sized for two full sets of every variant plus build scratch. The rebuild container has a memory limit so a build cannot take down PostGIS.
- **Observability.** JSON logs to stdout with a request id propagated to worker jobs, Docker log rotation. Prometheus-style `/metrics` on API and worker: request latency, routing latency, queue depth, job failures. Health checks: PostGIS `SELECT 1`, Valhalla a canary route between two fixed DC points per variant, worker a heartbeat row. External uptime check on `/healthz`. Alerts by email or an ops Discord webhook on: worker heartbeat silent for 10 minutes (independent of the backup alert, so a stalled worker is caught before the backup window), backup not succeeded in 26 hours, rebuild failed or not completed in 8 days, disk over 80 percent, canary route failing.
- **Host sizing and hardening.** Initial host: 8 vCPU, 32 GB RAM, 200 GB gp3. Compose memory limits: each Valhalla 2 GB, Photon 3 GB, PostGIS 4 GB, rebuild 8 GB, renderer 1 GB; the sum including swap-time duplicate Valhalla containers must stay under host RAM, verified in CI by a script that reads the compose limits. Security group allows 443 and 80 only, SSH via SSM Session Manager, unattended security updates, named volumes for PostGIS data, Caddy data for certificate persistence, tiles, and uploads.
- **Legal text.** ODbL and Protomaps attribution as in Region and data. A liability disclaimer that routes reflect map data and community reports, that jurisdiction information is community-maintained and not legal advice, and that riders are responsible for their own safety.

## Phases

1. **Foundation.** Preprocessing pipeline with stress classifier, jurisdiction tagger, sinuosity, border control node insertion and crossings table, Lua remap and its tests, segment table with staging swap; standard, mass ride, and e-bike tile variants built from the same extract; elevation tiles; PostGIS schema with jurisdiction polygons and override table; Procrastinate worker; weekly rebuild with validated swap; Photon; thumbnail renderer; Docker Compose with migrate service; Discord login with sessions and re-validation; the map with self-hosted PMTiles and the stress overlay.
2. **Routing and editing.** All modalities as data with user-facing controls, the editor with waypoints and control points, planning time and speed, versions with history, stats block including jurisdiction breakdown, GPX import with map matching, cue override layer, GPX export with device cue budgets, rider, handlebar, and marshal cue sheets, intersection list and overview map, visibility levels including public with the leak tests, owning guild and audience, named collaborators, share links with thumbnails, routes index and My routes. Reference routes are delivered before modality tuning starts.
3. **Jurisdiction.** Boundary overlay for all layers, ride purpose and warning settings with explicit-set flags, state line warnings from the crossings table and jurisdiction attributes, waypoint placement warnings, per-authority notice levels, control point notes, crossing list in summary and cue sheets, info cards, admin settings page for per-server default purpose and notice levels.
4. **Peer review.** Route states, proposals with splice merge and stats diff, approvals and re-pinning, anchored comments, issues with lifecycle, closures feeding the router, forking, Needs review queue, in-app notifications, explicit Discord posting, moderation tools including the override row proposal and approval UI, ownership transfer, reviewer penalties in the weekly rebuild. Release readiness checklist: attribution, disclaimer, and contributor licensing text; nightly backups verified and restore drill passed; account deletion; role mapping admin page; health checks and alerts live; rate limits on; visibility tests passing.

**First public release is the end of phase 4.** Everything a mass ride organizer needs to plan, check jurisdictions, produce marshal documents, and get the route reviewed is in by then, with download as the only delivery path.

5. **Integrations.** FIT and TCX export with device cue budgets, connected accounts, Wahoo and Hammerhead push, Garmin only if the developer program reopens.
6. **Costing fork.** Path-avoidance dial replacing the mass ride tile variant and lifting the variant exclusivity, and lit weighting in bicycle costing. Rebase procedure documented. Follow-up recorded after this phase: closed-course variant for permitted events.

Events with dates, pacing, and RSVPs are out of scope.

## Quality bar

- **Unit tests** for the stress classifier, the conflation step, the jurisdiction tagger and crossing rules, the border control node insertion and crossings table, the directional conditional access remap for the parkway reversal, the Lua remap producing the intended Valhalla attributes for each stress tier, the turn model and left-turn classifier, and the cue type mappings per format.
- **Reference routes**, supplied by the project owner before phase 2 tuning: at least one known good group ride loop, one past mass ride course, and one trail-heavy route. For each, routing its waypoints with its intended modality must reproduce at least a per-route percentage of the reference geometry by distance, initially 80. Per-modality invariants asserted on every reference route: Mass Ride includes zero trail-class distance; Group Ride includes zero distance on ways traversed at dismount speed and a trail-class share at most the reference route's own; Beginner includes zero top-tier stress distance, or where the router degraded to a penalty the stats block carries a gap warning and the flagged distance equals the top-tier distance; Recovery gain is at most Default gain; Mountain Goat gain is at least Default gain; Fast turn count is at most Default turn count.
- **Warnings default test.** Jurisdiction warnings depend only on the per-route setting, and its default is computed from modality and purpose exactly as in the table. Explicitly set values survive a modality change.
- **Visibility tests.** A private route is unreachable by share link, index, queue, webhook, and thumbnail. A reviewers-visibility route's thumbnail and metadata are unreachable without login. Only link-visibility routes serve OpenGraph data unauthenticated, in both `link_requires_login` modes. A reviewers-visibility route whose audience is guild A alone is unreachable by a reviewer whose only membership is guild B, and becomes reachable only after guild B is added to the audience, which writes an audit row. A user holding the reviewer role in one audience guild and none in another gets reviewer permission on the route. A draft never appears in the index, queue, or Discord posting, and a draft set to public behaves as link, staying out of the public index until it leaves draft. Only public routes appear in the public index and omit `noindex`. A guest can comment on a public route and can do nothing else, including on that same route: no fork, proposal, approval, or confirmation. With `public_guest_comments` off, or with it off for one route, a guest write is refused.
- **Export consistency test.** One compiled route exported as GPX, FIT, TCX, and each cue sheet yields identical ordered turn lists after applying the format's cue mapping.
- **End-to-end tests** for: create, save, share link; submit, compare, merge, and reject a proposal including the advanced-base case; comment anchoring surviving a version change; issue confirm feeding the penalty tag after a rebuild; a closure producing an alert on a crossing route; a request with explicit zero crossing costs paying nothing at a state line.
- **Fixtures.** A checked-in list of Potomac and Anacostia crossings with expected roadway and sidepath bike legality, asserted by the legality tagger test. A checked-in list of DC traffic circles (Dupont, Logan, Thomas, Scott, Washington, Columbus, Chevy Chase, Westmoreland, Grant, Sherman, Ward, Tenley, Sheridan, Barney, Randle, and the Lincoln Memorial circle) with expected cue text. Bike legality and roadway-versus-sidepath status verified for every downtown tunnel and circle underpass, every parkway, and every federal reservation in the coverage area, with corrections recorded in the override table and never presented in the UI as legal statements.
- **Latency.** p95 under 3 seconds for the single-pass preview of a 50-mile route on the production host, and under a per-modality configured bound for the full candidate set, with Valhalla calls for the set issued concurrently.

## Working conventions

- Ask before choosing between materially different approaches. Do not ask about routine implementation choices.
- Keep any Valhalla fork as a patch set with a documented rebase procedure.
- Document every external data source with its license, URL, and refresh procedure, and re-verify partner API terms before implementing each integration.
- Partner API registrations are project-owner tasks: Wahoo sandbox and production review, Hammerhead developer account and API license, Garmin only if the program reopens. Build against the download path first and stub the push adapters.
- Never present jurisdiction, permit, or access information in the UI as a legal statement.
