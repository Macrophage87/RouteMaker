**New**
- Plan starts as a loop on most ride types: the first point is the start and finish, each later tap is a stop, and the ride comes back. Untick Loop for a ride that ends somewhere else.
- To end a loop somewhere else, pick "Make this the destination" under a search result, right-click the map (press and hold on a phone) and choose Set as end, or End here on a station's card. The loop turns off and the ride finishes there. Undo puts the loop back.
- While you plan, the lines joining your points are bolder, on a white edge, with arrows the way you'll ride each leg. A loop shows its way back to the start too.
- Drag one of those lines to add a via point in that leg, as you already could on a planned route. A tap near a line still places your next point.
- With no points yet, Plan's Route button is "Start at current location".
- Manual mode can be turned on during a loop; it turns the loop off, since each tap is then the new end.
- Once your route is up to date and you've picked it, Plan's big button at the top of the map becomes Save route; signed out, it offers Discord sign-in or Download GPX. Change anything and it's Update route again (also in v0.10.2).
- On a screen at least 1200 px wide, Plan puts the route's results (totals, Start ride, GPX, Link and the folds) in their own card on the right of the map; the planner on the left keeps the points, the ride type, its settings and the Route button. The route's folds now open one at a time on every screen size.
- Plan's panel has fewer words on screen: the paragraphs under Loop, after the first point and under Start, and why Route is greyed out, are now said to screen readers only.
- Novice and Nervous walks you past a short busy stretch. Where its route still uses a road rated LTS 4 or worse for up to about 0.5 mi [800 m], and a sidewalk or path runs beside it, the directions say to walk your bike on the sidewalk there, and the ride says it ahead. The walk is drawn on the map, and the route's time counts it at walking pace. A road with no sidewalk is still ridden. Longer busy stretches are left to the transit offer.
- Novice and Nervous's "Own bike + transit" and "Bikeshare + transit" now also try hopping past the route's busiest stretch: ride to a station or stop near where it begins, take the train or bus past it, and ride on from near where it ends. The quickest such trip is planned, and its row says "skips the busy stretch".
- The short sidewalk on Redland Road in Derwood that joins the Lake Needwood path to the Redland Road side path is open to bikes (checked on site, 2026-10-21).
- Map: neighborhood and town names are now in an extra-bold Atkinson Hyperlegible (the street names' font family), dark on a white outline, instead of thin, widely spaced grey capitals. Towns are in normal case and a little larger; neighborhoods are in small capitals, so they read apart from street names (also in v0.10.2).
- Map: interstate (motorway) names and route shields are hidden until zoom 14, so the zoomed-out map is not dominated by roads bikes almost never use. Parkways, and US and state route numbers on ordinary main roads, still show.
- Beta testers' Discord forums: when a report's fix is merged, its post gets a reply saying which release it's in, and another when that release is live on beta, so you know when to try it.
- Map: the most-ridden bike corridors have a soft cyan band under them, wider for busier ones. They come from a year of Capital Bikeshare trips, routed over our streets, plus well-known routes such as Rock Creek, the Capital Crescent, the Mount Vernon Trail, Q and R Streets and Pennsylvania Avenue. The line inside still shows how stressful it is, and the legend has a Popular corridor row. Zoomed out, the map keeps these corridors and long calm routes, and long trails are named from zoom 10. Shown after the next map update.
- Map: where lines cross, the calmer road is drawn on top: LTS 1 over LTS 2, and both over the busy roads, so a trail is no longer cut by the highway it crosses. Among lines at the same level, an off-road path comes first, then a protected lane, a painted lane and a long calm street.
- Map: downtown at zoom 12-13 is less crowded. Water, restrooms and repair stands that would crowd each other are thinned until you zoom in, and bike lane rails on busy city roads wait for zoom 14, except on the popular corridors. Rural areas, where they are far apart, show every one.
- During a ride, the map puts your route and the way first: the corridor band is hidden and busy roads fade further, so the calm streets you might turn onto stand out.

**Fixed**
- DDOT's 2020 stress ratings no longer override a newer bike lane: where a bike lane or path is mapped and DDOT rated the road higher, our own rating is used, as on 14th Street NW. A painted lane where DDOT rated the road above LTS 2.5 is never rated calmer than LTS 2.5, as on parts of R Street NW. The road's details say which rating is used and why. Shown after the next map update.
- Default, and the other ride types that skip dirt trails, follow a new rule for them. A stop, via point or end you put on a dirt trail is routed to along it, and along the dirt trails joined to it. Starting on one is no longer enough on its own, because whether a trail is rideable depends on the weather and drainage. Once you're riding a dirt trail, or your route took you onto one, every re-route on that ride may use any dirt trail. These ride types still never plan onto a dirt trail on their own. Rated mountain-bike singletrack stays closed. Not on weekends (the weekend map has no dirt trails).
- If you press Road closed on a dirt trail during a ride on one of those ride types, the app takes it as a sign the trails are in bad shape. Re-routes for the rest of that ride stay off dirt trails, even a stop on one, and a new plan is how you opt back in. A road that only crosses a dirt trail doesn't count. Gravel, Mountain Goat and Mountain Bike are unchanged.
- Gravel and dirt now look different on the map and on your route. Gravel and other firm unpaved surfaces are grey, darker when busier. Dirt, grass and other natural surfaces, and rutted tracks, stay brown. Both keep the dotted center line. The mountain-bike trails that most ride types skip are now brown dashes instead of grey ones. The legend has a Gravel row and a Dirt row.
- The long trails, such as the Capital Crescent, the Metropolitan Branch Trail and the W&OD, now show from the map's widest zoom, as far out as the main roads, instead of only from zoom 10. They are drawn above the base map's highways. The Mass Ride map still starts at zoom 10.
- Farm tracks with nothing on the map showing they're open to the public are now closed to bikes: about 1,500 mi [2,400 km] of them, mostly across farms and private land. Shown after the next map update.
- Golf course cart paths are closed to bikes. Shown after the next map update.
- Zip-line and rope courses, such as Go Ape, are no longer drawn as paths on the map or offered to routes. Shown after the next map update.
- The app has a privacy page, at /privacy.
- The mark for cobblestones or rails on a route is now a dark circle with an amber "!", so it no longer looks like the triangle of a higher-stress junction. Its key in Layers reads across the whole row instead of a word or two a line.
