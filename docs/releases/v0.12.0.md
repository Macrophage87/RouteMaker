**New**
- Broadcast: a small button beside Start, in Plan and Go. It opens your phone's share sheet with a link to the route; once you send it, your ride starts and you are its leader. If something goes wrong (the route isn't ready, the share failed), it says so beside the button and nothing starts. Turn your dot off any time with Show me as a dot, under the riders count.
- Share ride now makes you the ride's leader too. Sharing the same route again, to another chat, keeps you the leader for both.
- Opening a Share ride or Broadcast link follows the leader: the map moves with the leader's dot, which has a wide ring round it. The leader's phone sends the route it is riding (dashed, so a re-route shows) and the path it has taken (solid) about every 2 minutes and whenever the route changes, so someone who opens the link late sees the whole path (except the part within 650 ft (200 m) in a straight line of where it started, as rides often start at home). The server keeps these in memory for 2 minutes only. You see the leader at once, before answering whether to show yourself. Moving or zooming the map stops following; Follow the leader, under the riders count, turns it back on. While you ride yourself, the map follows you instead. A link opened in Plan follows the leader too, so someone at home can watch the ride.
- On a Share ride or Broadcast ride, everyone who chooses to show their dot is shown on or off the route while they ride, not only within a quarter mile of it, so the leader and riders who get separated can find each other. Once your ride ends, the quarter-mile rule is back.
- Roam: in Go with nothing planned, the big button is Roam, a ride with no destination. It shows the map, the road you are on and up to 3 roads ahead with their distance and stress level, read from the map already on your phone. Detour finds the nearest water, restroom or food by bike; Ride here rides there and comes back to Roam when that ride ends. Broadcast, a round button on the Roam screen, shares a link so others can follow your dot (distances are in a straight line, as there is no route). The next sunrise and sunset show in the corner. Slide End roam to stop. Your position stays on your phone, except for a Detour search and the ride it starts, and, while you broadcast, your dot and the way you've ridden, which the server keeps for 2 minutes.
- Calmer and Flatter: two round buttons beside the ride, a car with a down arrow and a mountain with a down arrow. Calmer moves your traffic setting one step calmer and Flatter your hills setting one step flatter, and each finds a new route from where you are, going on ahead where it can, as Re-plan from here does. Press again for the next step, until the calmest or flattest. If there is no calmer or flatter way from where you are, it says "Same route". The change lasts for this ride; Plan keeps your settings as they were. Not shown on a route everyone shares.
- Ride alerts have two more choices on a group ride, "Lost, separated from the group" and "Wait up, regroup", and a Mass Ride has "Lost, separated from the ride".
- Alert, Other has a Speak button where your browser supports speech to text: say the issue and it fills the box; check it and tap Send. Your browser's maker (Google for Chrome, Apple for Safari) turns the recording into text.
- Privacy: ride data is kept in memory only, and for 2 minutes at most once it stops being sent. Ride alerts now last 2 minutes (they were 10), and a phone's random ride code and its alert counts are forgotten 2 minutes after it last checked in.

**Routing**
- Hills: the Hills slider is now a grade, in percent: set it to the steepest grade you can climb for several minutes in your lowest gears without labored breathing (1% to 15%; 8% in the middle). Not sure? Leave it where it starts. Every climb counts as the time it takes; when choosing a route, grades past yours count for more the harder they get, and a climb you'd have to push the bike up is a last resort. A heavier bike makes climbs slower but doesn't change how much steepness counts. Fast starts at 11%, Cargo Bike at 4%; Mountain Goat's slider is unchanged. No map rebuild needed.

**Map**
- The cyan band under the popular bike corridors is gone, and so is its legend row. A popular corridor now stands out in its own line instead: in a city it is among the first ways drawn as you zoom in, and it draws over the streets of its stress level.
- In a city the map now builds up like a road map does for cars, main routes first. At zoom 12 you see the off-road paths, protected bike lanes, the busiest bike corridors and the long calm streets that link them. Painted bike lanes and the next corridors join at zoom 13, and every other street, sidewalk and busy road at zoom 14 (the base map still shows the streets underneath). More of DC, Arlington and the close-in suburbs now count as city. Rural roads are drawn as before.
- Water, restroom and repair icons are spaced further apart until zoom 14, so downtown shows fewer at once; out in the country every one still shows.
- Short gaps are closed: where one or two short streets (up to about 650 ft in all) link two bike lanes, protected lanes or paths, the link is drawn from zoom 12, and a short gap in a popular corridor takes the corridor's class. Short stray pieces of a popular corridor that stand alone and run under about a quarter mile drop to a lesser class. Takes effect with the next map rebuild.
- Zoom 12-13 is tidier, in the city and out of it. Calm streets (LTS 1-2) that join a short bit of bike lane or path to the rest, and the crossings between two bike lanes, now show from zoom 12, and a street drawn at zoom 12-13 no longer breaks at a single busy block (up to about 820 ft). A short bit that joins nothing, and dead-end streets such as cul-de-sacs, wait for zoom 14. Takes effect with the next map rebuild.
- Zoomed out, the trails join up: the calmest streets between them (such as the Metropolitan Branch Trail to Rock Creek and to the Anacostia Riverwalk) and the minor trails that join them show zoomed out, with the long trails. Stray bits under a mile long that join nothing are left off at zoom 10-11. Takes effect with the next map rebuild.
- On- and off-ramps are left off the map until zoom 14, in the city and out of it, unless they have a bike lane or path or are a popular bike corridor. Takes effect with the next map rebuild.
- Zoomed out (zoom 10-11), a protected lane's rails are drawn as longer posts, so they no longer blur into a feathered fringe.
- The major corridors are named on the zoomed-out map, as the long trails are: Q Street, R Street, Pennsylvania Avenue, K Street, the 15th Street and Irving Street lanes and the other well-known corridors. Takes effect with the next map rebuild.
- The Irving Street bikeway now includes the Kenyon Street NW cycle track (11th Street NW to Irving) and the Irving track east of 1st Street NW, drawn and named as a major corridor. Takes effect with the next map rebuild.
- National Airport's Metro station is labelled "National Airport (DCA)", and the base map no longer repeats the airport's name beside it. Dulles and BWI lose their repeated airport label the same way. The station's card and links keep WMATA's name.
- Airports are closed to bikes like other working sites: their airside service roads and tracks are no longer drawn or routed. Public streets and the trails past the airport, such as the Mount Vernon Trail, stay. Takes effect with the next map rebuild.
- Connect Garmin, in Settings > Account: connect your Garmin account once, and Send to Garmin adds the route to Garmin Connect directly as a course, which syncs to your Edge or watch. Sending a saved route again updates its course instead of adding a copy. RouteMaker keeps only Garmin's sign-in token, encrypted, and can only send courses: it reads nothing from your Garmin but your account number. Disconnect in Settings or in Garmin Connect at any time; deleting your account does the same. Not connected, or when Garmin refuses the course, Send to Garmin hands the file to the phone as before. In the Android app, connecting needs the new app build. The privacy page's Garmin section now describes the live connection.

**Ride cues**
- A clear turn is said even where the way keeps its name: "Left to stay on Metropolitan Branch Trail" where the trail turns off onto its side path along a road. A turn of more than 45 degrees along one street or trail gets a cue wherever another way meets it.
- A turnaround always gets a cue, even onto a short unnamed path just before the next turn.
- A gentle bend in a trail is no longer said as a left or a right: turns are now read from the route's own line over about 65 ft (20 m) either side of a junction, so a tiny piece of path mapped at a crossing no longer tilts them.
