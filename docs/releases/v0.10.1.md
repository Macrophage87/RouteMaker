**New**
- Shared links show the RouteMaker icon. A link to the app shows the icon, the name and what the app does; a shared route shows its line, distance and climb with the icon beside "RouteMaker". This works in text messages, Discord and other chat apps, including on the beta, where only these previews (never the app itself) are open without the password.
- Hovering a mountain-bike trail or a climb with a mouse shows a small card, like a Metro station's: a trail's name, its difficulty and what that is based on, or a climb's length, gain and grade, and its elevation profile. A click opens the full card.
- Every climb and trail elevation profile shows the compass direction at each end (N ... S), and screen readers hear "Runs north to south."
- Climb dots and dashes are drawn under street names.
- Major off-road trails are named on the map, along the line like a street name, from the zoom where every street appears (the Metropolitan Branch Trail, Capital Crescent Trail, Rock Creek Trail and the like). A major trail is one the zoomed-out map already shows: on a regional or longer bike route or a long walking route, or a named trail at least 2.5 miles (4 km) long (5 miles, 8 km, if unpaved). The names are in the purple of the trail's edge lines, the street names' size and font, and 20 px and shortened ("Rock Creek Trl") in a ride. Street and station names win where they would overlap. Close in, every path's name is now purple too.
- The map's (i) button now reads "Help".
- The version number shows under the RouteMaker title in Plan (v0.10.1), in the browser tab's title and in the map's Help panel, so you can tell when the app has been updated.
- The documentation's diagrams fit a phone screen.

**Fixed**
- A sharp turn that curls round, such as a ramp up onto a bridge, is now said on the side you actually turn. Before, a hairpin right could be called a left.
- A short path across a road between two turns is now said as the crossing: "Cross Redland Road, then left onto Redland Road bike lane", on screen, by voice and to screen readers, with a straight-on arrow.
- A trail fork whose two branches both lean off (a Y) now gets its cue, such as "Bear left to stay on Rock Creek Trail". So does a trail that turns at a T.
- The Ride ended summary fits on the screen: each figure is one row, the elevation chart is sized to the screen, and Done and Undo are always in view. On a short or sideways screen only the figures scroll.
- A route to a Metro or MARC station with your own bike now ends at the elevator nearest where you're coming from, and the app checks which of the station's elevators is quickest to ride to, so it no longer sends you round the far side of the tracks. If it moves your point, it says so, and Undo puts it back. An elevator you tap on the map stays where you put it.
- Re-routes stop asking for a U-turn you keep riding past: after two "Make a U-turn" re-routes in a row, the third keeps your way ("New route found. Keep going ahead."). If there is still no way ahead (or the map is wrong there), it says "No route found ahead. Make a U-turn." once and leaves that route up; it then checks quietly about once a minute for a way ahead and only speaks if it finds one. Turning round as asked, or a re-route without a U-turn, starts the count again.
- After a re-route that turns you round, the ride card now shows the U-turn arrow until you've turned, not the turn after it.
- Default, and the other ride types that skip dirt trails, no longer turn you back off a dirt trail you're riding: a dirt trail you're on, or put a stop or via point on, is routed along, and so are the dirt trails joined to it. They still never plan onto a dirt trail on their own. Rated mountain-bike singletrack stays closed. Not yet on weekends (the weekend map has no dirt trails).

**Also arriving with this release's map**
- Mountain Bike routing works on the beta: its route server and the trail server Gravel and Mountain Goat use now start there.
- Parking aisles and driveways open to bikes are drawn on the stress map, such as Pennyfield Lock Road.
- Roads Maryland's State Highway Administration closes to bikes are left off the map and out of routes.
- Roads Montgomery County rates its most stressful show as very high stress (4.5) instead of Avoid.
- Traffic signals that DC, Maryland and Alexandria list but the map was missing now count as signals.
- Routes steer clear of cobblestones and rails in the road, such as Georgetown's O and P Streets and the H Street NE streetcar tracks, and warn you when they ride one.
- Climbs have names, from their roads and the named hill, mountain or gap they run up.
- Mountain Bike rides rated trails, and Gravel and Mountain Goat ride green (level 1) and unrated ones.

**Coming with the next map update**
- Routes can ride a sidewalk as a last resort. Beside a road rated 4.5 or Avoid, the sidewalk is taken over the road from Balanced up on the stress slider, and counts as LTS 3. Beside a calmer road, including LTS 4, the road is taken, and the sidewalk is used only where nothing else connects; there it counts as LTS 4. A crosswalk over a slip lane adds a wait to the route's time. DC's downtown sidewalks stay closed, as does a sidewalk mapped as private or impassable. Where a bike path stops on a sidewalk or at a crosswalk a few steps short of a bike lane, side path or street, routes now join them. One example is the path from Lake Needwood, which ends on a sidewalk about 120 ft (36 m) from the Redland Road side path.
- Routes can walk into Metro and MARC stations. The walking tunnels and the last footways between a station's entrances or elevator and the nearest road or bike path are now walk-your-bike links, so a route reaches the station from your side instead of stopping at the last road or going round to another entrance. The walk is at most about 800 ft (250 m), and the directions say "walk your bike here". Shady Grove's tunnel to the bus bays is one example.
- Escalators are closed to bikes: routes take the elevator.
