**New**
- Claude on the web and on your phone can plan and save RouteMaker routes for you: add RouteMaker as a connector and sign in with Discord. Settings, Account, AI assistants lists each app you've connected, with Disconnect.
- Routes an assistant plans open exactly as planned from its share link, and it can hand you a GPX file.
- Transit trips leaving now board the next Metro train, or the chosen trip's next Metrobus, that WMATA says you can reach in time, marked "live". The rest stays on the timetable.
- Star your favorite ride types and they're listed first whenever you choose a ride type, in Go's More ride types and in Plan. Signed in, they follow you to your other devices.
- Plan shows when manual mode is on, with "Manual mode: taps join with straight lines" under the map's bar. While you plan, faint lines join your points, solid where they'll be routed and dashed where you drew them, and drawn legs stay dashed on the route.
- A traffic light that only flashes, red for the side street while the main road keeps going, no longer counts as a signal. Routes price crossing the main road there as crossing from a stop sign, and the warning says "flashing light, not a signal". Maryland's flashing beacons are included, such as Connecticut Ave at Baltimore St in Kensington.
- Bikeshare docks sit on a white bubble on the map, so they stand out from the red and orange stress lines. Tap the bubble for the dock's card.
- Ride and the directions say when the route goes onto a service road, and name the main road it runs beside: "Left onto the service road beside East-West Highway." Unnamed sidewalks and paths beside a main road are named the same way, and alleys and parking aisles are called what they are.
- Route times count the stops: a stop sign adds about 8 seconds (more where you wait for a gap in a busier road: about 12 to 30), a slow-and-roll at a DC stop sign on a neighborhood street about 4, and a traffic signal about 15. Routes through a grid of stop signs are no longer timed as quick as a trail, and a smoother route of about the same length is now preferred.
- Street names on the map are larger, darker and on a white outline, so they stay readable over the colored stress lines. Every name on the map, from streets to trails and stations, is now set in Atkinson Hyperlegible Next.
- Ride's top row is just End ride and Road closed, side by side across the screen and one line at every phone text size, with Road closed always in words. Near sunrise or sunset a small line under them gives the times and the minutes till each, such as "Sunset 6:22 PM (22 min)".
- Texting DC 311 is gone, since DC's text line only ever answered "Welcome". Instead, the 311 button logs the problem anywhere on your ride, and you can also right-click a road (press and hold on a phone) and choose Log for 311. Your 311 checklist in Suggest groups everything by the website that takes it, such as DC 311, MC311, PGC311 or VDOT, with Copy report. Check an item off to archive it.
- Ride has a Large text button (Aa) on its side: the next turn as big as it fits on the top half of the screen, for bright sun or near sight. Where am I? also re-centres the map on you. In a ride, street names on the map are larger and shortened, such as "R St NW", and the turn card uses fewer words with bigger type and a bigger arrow. End ride and Road closed have larger text.
- At an unusual junction, such as a five-way or a left onto a service road, Ride's turn card draws the junction instead of a plain arrow: your way solid, the other roads dashed, and a service road's main road where it lies. At a traffic circle the card draws the ring with your exit's number in it, and the cue counts the exits: "Take the 3rd exit onto P Street Northwest at Dupont Circle Northwest." Ordinary crossroads keep the plain arrow.
- Go draws your route, More direct and Trailmaxxed together on the map, each named where it leaves the others with what it trades against yours, such as "+6 min, −1.2 mi (1.9 km) high-stress". Press a faint route to choose it. Calmer is now Trailmaxxed: your ride type at the top of the traffic slider.
- Tapping a stop (water, restroom, Metro, food, repair or a sight) opens its card, with whether it's open now, how far it is or the detour, and Route here or Add as stop. Nothing is routed until you choose.
- Mountain-bike trails can be tapped for their difficulty, an elevation profile and Route through this trail. Each map key now sits inside its own section of Layers, and the Legend button is gone.
- Settings fills the screen on a phone, in the order My Go, Riding, Display, Account, one row per setting.
- Your rider and bike weight is always kept on this device, with no Remember box. Signed in, it follows you to your other devices.
- End ride shows your ride's summary: distance, moving time, average speed, total time, climbing, an elevation chart and the traffic stress you rode, in large text. Done leaves Go empty for your next trip instead of setting the same ride up again.

**Coming with the next map update**
- Parking aisles and driveways open to bikes are drawn on the stress map, such as Pennyfield Lock Road.
- Roads Maryland's highway agency (MDOT SHA) closes to bikes are left off the map and out of routes.
- Roads Montgomery County rates LTS 5 show as LTS 4.5, a very high-stress road, instead of Avoid.
- Traffic signals that DC, Maryland and Alexandria list but the map was missing now count as signals, so those crossings are no longer called unsignalised.
- Routes steer clear of cobblestones and rails in the road, such as Georgetown's O and P Streets and the H Street NE streetcar tracks. When a route does ride one, an amber triangle marks it, the directions name it, and Ride warns you ahead.
- Climbs have names, from their roads and the named hill, mountain or gap they run up. The name heads the climb's card and its line in your route's climbs list.
- Mountain Bike rides rated trails, and Gravel and Mountain Goat ride green (level 1) and unrated ones. A trail's panel gives its surface in detail, and climbs are colored by what they're ridden on: road, paved path, gravel or mountain-bike trail.
