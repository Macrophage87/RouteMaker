**New**
- Weather along your route: in Plan and Go, the route's warnings now include any National Weather Service alert in force along it while you would ride it if you left now, each with an NWS notice link, and a rain line such as "Rain expected in about 20 min, near mile 5.0 (8.0 km)." from Open-Meteo's 15-minute forecast. In a ride it is checked every 5 minutes along the rest of the route: a new alert or rain coming is said once and stays in the bottom panel until you press the big Dismiss button. Roam checks where you are, for the next hour. The server rounds each point to about 0.7 mi (1.1 km) before asking, so neither service learns where you are, and keeps the answers 2 minutes in memory only. Start ride never waits for it.
- Featured routes, behind Go's More: rides written up around one exact route, those in season first. Each is a page of its own you can share, with the route's figures, map, charts, a small map of each highlight, Open in RouteMaker, Download GPX, Send to Garmin and Send to Wahoo. The route is kept as it was when the post was written.
- Stops and via points are now separate. A tap on the map adds a via point, a small ring that only shapes the route: no number, no cue. Ctrl+click (Cmd+click on a Mac) or Add as stop adds a numbered stop and asks for a name you can leave blank; Add as stop on a place's card, or a place picked in Add a stop, names it after the place. A stop's name goes with it into links, saved routes, GPX, FIT and TCX files, Garmin, Wahoo and the ride's cues ("You have reached stop 2, Union Market."). In a GPX file you open, named points become stops and unnamed ones via points.
- Resume your ride: if the app closes during a ride, opening it again within 12 hours offers Resume ride, from where you are. Only the planned route and stops are kept, on your device only, never on the server; End ride removes them.
- Already done?: starting again part way along a route lists its stops with the ones behind you ticked. Untick one to go back to it, or tick one ahead to skip it.

**Ride**
- Start ride in Plan now starts at once, as in Go, with RouteMaker's voice saying every turn until you change it in Settings, Riding, or in More during a ride.
- The ride screen's round buttons are now Where am I?, Large text, Broadcast, Detour, 311 and More. Broadcast sends a ride link from a ride already under way and makes you its leader. More holds Less traffic and Fewer hills (formerly Calmer and Flatter), Skip this stop, and the ride's settings.
- Detour has four buttons, Water, Restroom, Food and Repair (repair stands and bike shops), each listing the three nearest by bike, and a Search box for anything else ("coffee", "grocery", an address). With a stop ahead, a place can replace that stop ("Instead of stop 2").
- The stops still ahead are drawn on the ride's map with their numbers and names. When the ride plans again it keeps every stop ahead, but drops a via point less than half a mile (800 m) ahead, so you are never sent back to touch it.
- Left alone for 30 seconds, the ride and Roam map goes back to following you, and map cards, the road info and the side menus close. Anything you are typing in or that asks you a question stays open; in accessibility mode nothing closes on its own. A map card's close button is bigger.
- Street and trail names in a ride are placed before shop names and the water, restroom and repair icons, so the streets around you, your route included, keep their names. Station names and hazard marks still come first.
- Ride cues: where the bike lane to turn onto is on the far side of a road, the cue says to cross first: "Cross Saint Paul Street, then right onto Saint Paul Street bike lane".

**Routing**
- No more U-turns around the end of a median: a route no longer turns from one side of a divided road onto the other where the median ends (such as Frederick Road at the Beltway in Catonsville). Takes effect with the next map rebuild.
- Baltimore Penn Station: rides now go to the front doors on the drop-off loop off N Charles St, not past them to a point by the tracks.

**Map**
- Baltimore Penn Station's card gives the next MARC train both ways, southbound included. MARC lines are named "Penn Line" and so on once the timetable next refreshes.
