How RouteMaker works

RouteMaker plans bike routes between Washington, DC and Baltimore that keep you away from heavy traffic. It rates every road and path for how stressful its traffic feels on a bike, then looks for the route with the least stress for the kind of ride you chose.

Every road gets a stress level

RouteMaker uses Level of Traffic Stress (LTS), a four-level scale for bike riding developed by Mekuria, Furth and Nixon[1] and refined by Furth[2]. LTS 1 suits children and LTS 4 only the most traffic-tolerant riders. RouteMaker adds LTS 4.5 and Avoid for the worst roads, and half steps for roads between two levels.

Which rating wins

A road's level comes from the first of these that rates it: community corrections, approved by hand; a local agency (Montgomery County or the District); a state agency (Maryland's State Highway Administration); then RouteMaker's own formula. Every place publishes different data, so the evidence behind a rating changes when you cross a county or state line. More: which rating wins and why and what each place tells us.

From ratings to a route

A route is chosen by adding up what every stretch, junction and closure costs, in calm miles: a mile of quiet street counts as 1, and a busier mile counts as more, as if it were longer. Busy junctions cost extra too, so a quiet street that crosses a highway at a stop sign is not treated as quiet. More: what each level costs, everything a route pays for, junctions and a worked example.

Ride types

Your ride type and the traffic slider decide how much extra distance a calmer road is worth, how fast you are assumed to ride, and how hills and surfaces count. They change the costs, never the ratings. More: every ride type compared. Three ride types use methods of their own:

More: Ride types
  • Mass Ride, for a large group that takes the lane, plans by how many riders a road can carry and where corkers are needed, not by traffic stress.
  • Mountain Bike, Mountain Goat and Gravel seek trails, climbs or unpaved roads, using trail ratings and elevation.

Closures

Real closures move a route: state work zones, police and city alerts, and closures a reviewer has confirmed. DC's planned-work permits never do. More: closures.

While you ride

Turn cues come before each turn, and if you leave the route RouteMaker plans on from where you are, in the direction you are heading. The 311 button, and Log for 311 when you right-click a road, add a problem to your 311 checklist in Suggest, under the website that takes it. More: while you ride.

Corrections and community input

Map data is not always right. Riders who are signed in can suggest a level from the road panel. Every suggestion is reviewed and never applied automatically. Each weekly map rebuild produces a report of every rating that changed, by county, before the new map goes live. More: corrections and reporting a wrong rating.
