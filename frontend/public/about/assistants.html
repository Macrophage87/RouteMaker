AI assistants

RouteMaker can be used from an AI assistant such as Claude. You describe the ride you want in your own words, and the assistant plans it with RouteMaker's own planner, shows you a link to look at it in the app, changes it when you ask, and saves it when you say so: to My routes on the web, or as a GPX file on your own computer.

At a glance

  • Ask for a route in your own words, for example a 100 mile loop round DC on low-stress roads that never strays more than 5 miles from Metro.
  • The assistant plans through the same route request as the app, so the route is exactly what the app would plan.
  • Every route is a draft with a link to open it in the app. Nothing is saved until you say so.
  • For riders, the connection can only add routes to My routes. It cannot read or change anything else. Instance admins can also have it write draft Featured routes posts.

What it is

RouteMaker speaks the Model Context Protocol[1], an open standard that lets an AI assistant use outside tools. Any assistant app that supports it can plan routes with RouteMaker, not only Claude. There are two ways in: Claude on the web and the phone apps connect to the site itself, and assistant apps on your own computer can run RouteMaker's server there[2].

More: What it is

Either way, the assistant goes through the same route request the app makes[3], so a route has the same ratings, costs, closures and ride types as one planned in the app. On the web each rider's requests count against their own limits, as in the app.

Asking for a route

Ask in your own words, look at the draft in the app, ask for changes, and say save when it is right. The assistant chooses the ride type and settings that fit what you ask, and you can name them yourself.

More: Asking for a route

Things you might ask:

  • "Make me a 100 mile route that goes around DC using low-stress roads and doesn't stray more than 5 miles from a Metro station."
  • "Plan a calm ride from Union Station to the National Arboretum on a Capital Bikeshare e-bike."
  • "Go by the Capital Crescent Trail instead." "Make it calmer." "Save it as Metro century."

Every ride type the app has is available, bikeshare and bike plus transit included, with every setting: traffic stress, hills, target distance, ride time, gravel and trails, rider weight, cargo, trail levels, the bikeshare bike and where to end, and departure time. Each answer gives the distance, time, climbing and how much of the route is on each stress level, the roads it takes in order (with miles, stress level and designations such as Montgomery County Rustic Roads, scenic byways, the East Coast Greenway and US Bicycle Routes), the busy stretches on stress level 3 and above, the very-high-stress junctions and any small detours, so the assistant can tell you the busy parts before you open the route, and a link, kept for a week, that opens that exact route in the app. You can change the route in the app yourself and paste the page's address back: the assistant picks it up as a new draft. On the traffic slider, 70 is the app's Default and 80 keeps to low-stress ways unless avoiding them takes much longer; above 80 the calm search runs, which is slower on a long ride.

A place you name is looked for near the route's points that are already known (coordinates, Metro stations or places found before it), so "Moore Road" means the one near your ride. If a name matches places far apart and none is near those points, the assistant asks you which one you mean; with nothing yet to go by, it takes the search's first match and says what else the name could have been. Where the route would reach a via point by riding out and back 0.2 mi [0.3 km] or more, the point is moved to where that spur leaves the road and the route is planned again, and the new plan is kept if it comes out shorter (and no farther from Metro when you set a Metro limit). When planning or revising a route you can ask to keep the points where they are.

What an assistant can do

The assistant has eleven tools, and instance admins get a twelfth. Only save_route adds to My routes, and only when you ask it to save; an admin's draft_route_post writes a draft post that only admins see.

More: What an assistant can do
What an assistant can do
ToolWhat it does
find_placeSearches RouteMaker's place search for a place, address or street, around a point if given.
metro_stationsLists the 98 Metrorail stations, or those near a point, with miles and direction.
scenic_roads_nearLists the Montgomery County Rustic Roads, scenic byways, East Coast Greenway and US Bicycle Routes within 5 mi [8 km] of a point, or up to 15 mi [24 km] if asked, nearest first and at most 25, each with its miles in reach, its miles on each stress level and a point on it to route through. So a ride on rustic roads can be asked for in words alone.
climbs_nearLists the categorised climbs near a point, with name, miles, gain, grade and surface. For interval training it keeps those long enough for one effort, steepest first, and plans up the first few to check that an effort fits with no stop, signal, busy crossing or sharp turn: hill repeats, up for the effort and down to recover.
closures_nearLists the trail and road closures and construction in force now near a point, nearest first: what is closed, until when, how far away, the source and its link. So an assistant can check a park or trail before suggesting a ride there.
plan_routePlans through 2 to 25 points (places, station names or coordinates) on any ride type and setting, as a loop if asked. Reports distance, time, climbing and the share on each stress level, and the bikeshare or transit plan on those ride types. Can check the distance from Metro and add stations where the route strays. Its answer, like every other plan's, also lists what the route rides: its roads in order with miles, stress level and any scenic designation, where it is on stress level 3 or above, its very-high-stress junctions, and small detours where it leaves a road and comes back to it. It also lists the closures on and around the route and warns whenever the route rides through one (a blocked bike lane is listed, not warned about), naming any stop or via point that sits on a closed stretch, and lists the notices to check before you go at the ride's time, such as park alerts and the parks' deer-management closures. For interval training it can check where efforts can run with no stop, signal, busy crossing, sharp turn, descent or stress level 4 road, how many sets fit in one session, and which climbs suit up-and-back repeats. For an alleycat or errands it can keep the start and finish and ride the checkpoints between in the quickest order.
revise_routeChanges a draft (add, move or remove points; another ride type or setting) and plans it again as a new draft. The old one stays.
open_linkPicks up a route from a RouteMaker link you paste: the app's address after you changed a route, a saved route opened in the app, or a shared route's link.
save_routeOn the web, saves a draft to My routes; on your own computer, writes it as a GPX file. Only when you say so.
gpx_routeGives a draft as a GPX track: a file on your computer, or a download link on the web.
draft_route_postInstance admins only, on the web: writes a Featured routes post as a draft around a draft route or a line to follow, for an admin to publish (see below).
plan_metro_loopBuilds a loop of about a target length, 5 to 200 mi [8 to 322 km], that stays within a set distance of a Metro station, 5 mi [8 km] unless you say otherwise.

Loops near Metro

One tool builds a loop of about the length you ask for that stays near Metro, so you can bail out by train. Distances to Metro are in a straight line, from the same station file the map uses[4].

More: Loops near Metro
  • The loop is centred on DC unless you name another centre or a start.
  • On Default it plans at traffic slider 80, low stress, unless you ask otherwise; other ride types keep their own starting setting. Mass Ride and the two-point ride types do not plan loops.
  • The site plans at most 124 mi [200 km] of straight lines between points, so past about 150 mi [241 km] the ring is kept smaller and the loop comes out short. The answer says so.
  • A point the loop reaches only by going out and back is moved to where the spur leaves the loop.

What it can and cannot see

For riders, the connection can only add. It can plan routes and add the ones you ask it to save to My routes; it cannot read your saved routes or change anything else. The server on your own computer saves nothing to the site at all.

More: What it can and cannot see
  • On the web, the connection uses OAuth[5]: your assistant app registers itself, you sign in to RouteMaker with Discord as usual and choose Allow, and the app gets a token for routes only. RouteMaker keeps only SHA-256 fingerprints of the tokens, never the tokens themselves. A token lasts an hour and is renewed while you use it; a connection unused for 30 days has to be connected again.
  • On your own computer, the server does not sign in, so it cannot save to My routes: it plans and writes GPX files. To keep a route, open its link and press Save in the app. The save-only key it once used is retired.
  • Instance admins can also have the connector write a Featured routes post, always as a draft that only admins see. Publishing it is still an admin's choice in the admin, and the connector cannot change a post once it has left draft.
  • Your conversation is between you and your assistant app, under that app's own terms. RouteMaker sees the routes it is asked to plan, as it does for the app.

Connecting an assistant

Most riders want the connector: on claude.ai or a Claude phone app, add RouteMaker as a connector and sign in, and it plans routes and saves the ones you ask for to My routes. Running the server on your own computer, from a copy of the code, plans the same routes but only writes GPX files. The table compares the two.

On claude.ai and the Claude phone apps

  1. In Claude, open Settings > Connectors > Add custom connector.
  2. Name it RouteMaker and give the address you open RouteMaker at, followed by /mcp (for example https://routes.example.org/mcp).
  3. Choose Connect. RouteMaker's sign-in opens: sign in with Discord as usual and choose Allow.

Settings > Account > AI assistants lists each connected assistant with a Disconnect button.

The assistant can then plan routes and save the ones you ask for to My routes.

Featured routes posts, for instance admins

Signed in as an instance admin, the connector also lets you ask the assistant to write a Featured routes post (title, about, who it is for, season, and highlighted sections with their miles) and send it to the site[3]. The post always lands as a draft: only admins see its page, and you publish it yourself in the admin under Status > Published, where you can change anything first.

  • The route is a draft the assistant planned or opened from a link, or a line to follow, such as a ride's GPX track, planned the way the app plans an opened file.
  • The admin's own checks apply: the route needs its planned figures, and one that starts, ends or stops in your Home circle is refused.
  • Asking again with the same post address (the end of its /routes/ link) and replace rewrites that draft. Without replace, a draft already there, such as one you started in the admin, is left alone. Once a post has left draft, the connector cannot change it.
  • The assistant can describe photos you showed it, each with alt text, an optional caption and the section it goes under, but cannot send the pictures. Each described photo waits on the draft's page, under For admins, Photos, for an admin to add its file.
  • Disconnecting the assistant in Settings ends it.

On your own computer (Claude Desktop, Claude Code and others)

The server needs Python 3.11 or later and a copy of the repository; it uses nothing beyond Python itself. Add the server to your assistant with these settings:

  • ROUTEMAKER_BASE_URL: the address you open RouteMaker at. Route links are built from it.
  • ROUTEMAKER_ROUTES_DIR (optional): where GPX files go, ~/RouteMaker-routes unless set.
  • ROUTEMAKER_BASIC_AUTH (optional): user:password for a site behind a password.

For Claude Code the command is:

claude mcp add routemaker \
  -e ROUTEMAKER_BASE_URL=https://routes.example.org \
  -- python3 /path/to/RouteMaker/mcp/routemaker_mcp.py

Claude Desktop and other apps take the same command, path and settings in their own configuration. The server's README[2] has a Claude Desktop example, including Windows with the copy in WSL.

Limits

The planner covers DC to Baltimore, and a long loop can take a while: about a minute per request on the web, a few minutes on your own computer.

More: Limits
  • Coverage. Points outside the DC to Baltimore area are refused.
  • Time. Each plan of a long ride can take up to a minute. On the web a request answers within about a minute, so a long loop may be planned in fewer rounds; the answer says when it stopped early, and asking for a revision can still fix it. On your own computer, if the assistant gives up first, raise its tool timeout (Claude Code: MCP_TOOL_TIMEOUT=600000) or ask for fewer plans. A request the assistant gives up on keeps nothing.
  • Changes in between. If closures or the map change between planning and opening the route in the app, a route planned again may differ a little. A saved route keeps the line exactly as planned.
