Ride types

A ride type tells RouteMaker what kind of trip you are making. Each one sets how much extra distance a calmer road is worth, how it treats hills and unpaved surfaces, and how fast you are likely to ride. All of them use the same stress ratings.

At a glance

  • Transportation: Default, Novice and Nervous, E-bike, Cargo Bike, Bikeshare, Own Bike + transit, Bikeshare + transit.
  • Adventure: Fast, Trailmaxxing, Gravel, Mountain Goat, Mountain Bike, Rebalance Ride.
  • Group: Group Ride and Mass Ride.
  • Go shows the four most common first: Default, Novice and Nervous, Bikeshare, and Own Bike + transit.

Looking for something else? How the costs work: Routing. The off-road ride types in depth: Mountain Bike, Mountain Goat and Gravel. Big group rides: Mass Ride.

Paces are speeds on smooth, flat roads, only for estimating times. Riding speed, in the ride settings under the Hills slider, puts your own pace in place of the ride type's: Ride type's speed (the default) or 8 to 30 mph [13 to 48 km/h]. Mass Ride, Bikeshare, Rebalance and Bikeshare + Transit keep their own pace and don't show it. A whole ride averages slower: climbs, rough surfaces and waits at junctions all add time (How a route's time is worked out). The Hills column is the grade the Hills slider starts at: the steepest you climb comfortably (Hills). "Surfaces" describes Valhalla's bike costing settings[1] that each ride type sends[2].

Bike + transit

Two ride types combine a bike with Metro, buses or trains: Own Bike + transit takes your bike on board, and Bikeshare + transit docks a shared bike before you board. Each plan rides to a station or stop and takes the train or bus the rest of the way.

  • Systems: Metro and Metrobus[4], MARC, Light RailLink and Metro SubwayLink[5], and VRE[6].
  • On a trip leaving now, each Metro step boards the next train WMATA predicts on its line and direction that you can reach the station in time for. Metrobus steps on the chosen trip board their next predicted bus. Those departures say "live"; the time on board stays the timetable's. A step with no prediction you can catch keeps its timetable time and says "scheduled". MARC, Light RailLink and VRE are always scheduled. The plan's total reads "Door-to-door time, with live departures".
  • Live Metro data also changes which trips are offered and their order. With Own Bike + transit, a trip that takes your bike through a station WMATA reports closed is dropped, unless every trip would be. A street elevator out at a station with no working spare, or a platform elevator out, moves the trip down the list as if it took 15 minutes longer, without changing its time, and the trip shows an elevator-out icon. Elevators in a station's parking garage or lot are ignored. Live arrivals, incidents and elevator outages are shown beside the plan. An elevator outage is a warning, never a reason to skip a station.
  • Limits: walk up to 1 mi [1.6 km] to a station or 0.4 mi [0.6 km] to a bus stop; ride up to 5 mi [8 km] to a station. A trip under 1 mi [1.6 km] is offered as walking.
  • Trips are ranked by when you get there, with time on busy roads and waiting at junctions counting against a trip, and ranked again once the chosen trip is routed. Each trip says how far you would ride on busy roads, which is why the list is not always in strict time order.
  • Every Metro station takes a bike; steps say "by the elevator" only where an elevator is known. Metro is not used on days when bikes are not allowed on trains, such as July 4.
  • With a bike, a start, stop or end you put at a Metro or MARC station moves to the station's elevator, or where none is known its entrance, quickest to ride to from where that leg comes from, not the far side of the tracks. If that moves your point, the app says so, and Undo puts it back. An elevator you tap on the map stays where you put it.
  • Routes walk into Metro and MARC stations: the walking tunnels and the last footways between a station's entrances or elevator and the nearest road or bike path are walk-your-bike links, at most about 800 ft [250 m], and the directions say "walk your bike here", and, from the same update, escalators are closed to bikes. From the next map update, an unnamed walkway that OpenStreetMap opens to bikes (bicycle=yes or permissive, not designated), at most 500 ft [150 m] long, that reaches a rail platform in service is walked too, not ridden as a bike path, and is no longer drawn as one: at Kensington's MARC station, the ramp to the platform, the crossing over the tracks and the walkways by the station building.
  • Own Bike + transit never leaves the bike. Bikeshare + transit docks the shared bike before you board.

How much a busy road costs

RouteMaker compares routes in calm miles: a mile of quiet street counts as 1, and a busier mile counts as more, as if it were longer. The traffic slider and the ride type together decide how much more. Routing has the cost of each level on every ride type, and everything else a route pays for.

More: How much a busy road costs

Above 80 on the slider, RouteMaker's own search also weighs each route's exposure to busy roads. Ride types differ in how heavily they count LTS 4 and Avoid:

Transportation ride types

For getting somewhere: Default for everyday riding, Novice and Nervous for riders new to traffic, and E-bike for assisted riding and Cargo Bike for a long, heavy bike. Bikeshare and Bike + transit have their own sections.

More: Transportation ride types

Default

Everyday riding that prefers paths and quiet streets, even when a busier road would be faster. The slider starts at 70: a mile of LTS 3 costs about 3 to 6 calm miles and a mile of LTS 4 about 13 to 16. Traffic-free paths and protected bike lanes count for a little less than a quiet street, so routes lean toward them.

Novice and Nervous

For riders new to traffic. Every road and junction is read one level busier than it is, so LTS 3 is treated as LTS 4, and busy roads are avoided hard. Detours are kept shorter than on Trailmaxxing (at most 1.4 times the length of the router's quickest route). Where a route still has to use real LTS 3 or worse, or the detour limit is reached, RouteMaker offers transit and bikeshare instead. Where a route still rides a road rated LTS 4 or worse for up to about 0.5 mi [800 m] and a sidewalk or path runs beside it, the directions say to walk your bike on the sidewalk there, the ride says so ahead, the walk is drawn on the map, and the route's time counts it at walking pace. A road with no sidewalk is still ridden. Novice and Nervous's Own Bike + transit and Bikeshare + transit offers also try riding to a station or stop near where the busiest stretch begins, taking the train or bus past it and riding on; that trip's row says "skips the busy stretch". Grades past 5%, uphill or downhill, are marked on the map and avoided where they can be, as on Mass Ride (Steep grades).

E-bike

Leaves out ways mapped as closed to e-bikes, starts the Hills slider at 12%, so a more direct route over a hill wins where it is quicker, and times trips at an assisted 15 mph [24 km/h].

Cargo Bike

A long, heavy bike. Gentle grades, few gates or barriers (each costs about 5 minutes, against 30 seconds on Default), and stronger avoidance of rough surfaces. Choose carrying cargo or with passengers; passengers start the slider at 80 and count LTS 2 streets a little, so routes lean toward LTS 1. Electric assist switches to the e-bike map and a faster pace, but hills still count.

Bikeshare

More: Bikeshare

Bikeshare

Capital Bikeshare only. Choose a classic bike (slower, avoids hills) or an e-bike. Walking speed is 3 mph [4.8 km/h]. Stations that have not reported for 30 minutes are ignored. A dock that adds less than 165 ft [50 m] of riding counts as "on your way". The station lists always show the three best stations, with e-bike counts.

Rebalance Ride

Moves a bikeshare bike from a crowded station to one that needs bikes. Rides like this can earn reward points (Bike Angels in the Capital Bikeshare app); the points show there, not in RouteMaker.

Adventure ride types

For riding for its own sake: Fast for direct roads, Trailmaxxing for the calmest route, Gravel, Mountain Goat and Mountain Bike off the pavement, and Rebalance Ride for moving a bikeshare bike to a station that needs one.

More: Adventure ride types

Fast

Direct roads, few turns and smooth pavement, whatever the traffic. The slider starts at 10, so a mile of LTS 4 costs only about 2 calm miles.

Trailmaxxing

The calmest route, aimed at your target distance: paths, protected lanes and quiet streets, with busy streets only where there is no other way. It finds the least stressful route, not the most trail, so it may add miles. With a target distance it aims at or under it and goes over only where the extra miles buy a real cut in stress, never past 1.25 times the target. With no target it stays within 1.6 times the router's quickest route (and at least 1 mi [1.6 km] more).

Gravel

Unpaved roads and trails cost nothing extra. It does not yet seek them out.

Mountain Goat

Looks for climbs: picks the hilliest of a few alternative routes, up to half again as long. Climbs are categorised by length times grade, from Category 5 to Hors Catégorie[9].

Mountain Bike

Seeks mountain-bike trails up to the level you choose (1 to 4, default 2; unrated trails count as 2), joined by calm roads: LTS 2 freely, LTS 3 only for gaps under 0.5 mi [0.8 km]. A trail mile counts as half a road mile, or 0.4 at your preferred level. It plans a loop from your start unless you turn the loop off, never uses trails closed to bikes or mapped closed when wet, and links to MORE's trail conditions[7].

Mountain Bike, Mountain Goat and Gravel each work differently from the road ride types: Mountain Bike, Mountain Goat and Gravel explains how.

Group ride types

For riding together: Group Ride for a group riding with traffic, and Mass Ride for a large group that takes the roadway.

More: Group ride types

Group Ride

A group riding with traffic at riding pace: quiet roads, fewer turns (each change of street costs 30 seconds, against 5 on Default) and fewer gates.

Mass Ride

A large group taking the roadway, such as a bike party: roads only, no trails, no bike-lane preference, never against a one-way street's traffic, few turns, at a parade pace of 6 mph [9.7 km/h]. No loops. The slider is locked at 0, because a group of hundreds controls its lane. Its map shows how many riders a minute each road can carry instead of traffic stress, for a ride of 100 to 2,000 riders[8], along with notes on federal land and reviewed hazard reports. Its method is unlike any other ride type's: Mass Ride explains it.

Options for any ride type

Two sliders and a few switches work on any ride type: how much traffic you accept, how you feel about hills, and whether to avoid gravel or trails.

More: Options for any ride type
  • Traffic slider: named notches from Traffic tolerant (10) through Direct (25), Balanced (40), Prefers quiet (70), Low-stress (80) and Calm (90) to Calmest (100).
  • Hills slider: the steepest grade you can climb for several minutes in your lowest gears without labored breathing, 1% to 15%. Every climb counts as the time it takes, and grades past yours count for more. Mountain Goat alone keeps its right half to seek climbs. A route never gets busier just because you set a lower grade.
  • Avoid gravel strongly prefers pavement on any ride type.
  • Keep to roads, not trails uses the roads-only map.
  • Weekend rides treat roads closed to cars at weekends as paths.
  • Go's route choice draws up to two more routes beside yours, on ride types other than transit, Rebalance Ride and Mass Ride: More direct, up to two notches more tolerant of traffic, shown only when it saves at least a minute, and Trailmaxxed, planned at the top of the traffic slider, shown only when it takes at least 250 ft [80 m] off busy riding. Press a line or its row to ride it; your own setting stays as it was.
