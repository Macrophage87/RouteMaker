Ride types

A ride type tells RouteMaker what kind of trip you are making. Each one sets how much extra distance a calmer road is worth, how it treats hills and unpaved surfaces, and how fast you are likely to ride. All of them use the same stress ratings.

At a glance

  • Transportation: Default, Novice and Nervous, E-bike, Cargo Bike, Bikeshare, Rebalance Ride, Own Bike + transit, Bikeshare + transit, Walk, Walk + transit.
  • Adventure: Fast, Road Bike, Tandem, Trailmaxxing, Gravel, Mountain Goat, Mountain Bike, Run.
  • Group: Group Ride and Mass Ride.
  • Go shows the four most common first: Default, Novice and Nervous, Bikeshare, and Own Bike + transit.

Looking for something else? How the costs work: Routing. The off-road ride types in depth: Mountain Bike, Mountain Goat and Gravel. Big group rides: Mass Ride.

Paces are speeds on smooth, flat roads, only for estimating times. Riding speed, in the ride settings under the Hills slider, puts your own pace in place of the ride type's: Ride type's speed (the default) or 8 to 30 mph [13 to 48 km/h]. Mass Ride, Bikeshare, Rebalance and Bikeshare + Transit keep their own pace and don't show it. A whole ride averages slower: climbs, rough surfaces and waits at junctions all add time (How a route's time is worked out). The Hills column is the grade the Hills slider starts at: the steepest you climb comfortably (Hills). "Surfaces" describes Valhalla's bike costing settings[1] that each ride type sends[2].

Bike + transit

Two ride types combine a bike with Metro, buses or trains: Own Bike + transit takes your bike on board, and Bikeshare + transit docks a shared bike before you board. Each plan rides to a station or stop and takes the train or bus the rest of the way.

Walk, Walk + transit, both bike + transit ride types and Bikeshare take up to 3 stops between the start and the end, and no via points. Each leg is planned as a trip of its own, leaving as you reach the stop before it. At a stop, once that leg is done, Start the next leg plans the rest again from where you are, leaving then. On Bikeshare you return the bike at each stop. The map and the route are the trip to stop 1; the Bikeshare panel lists each later trip's steps under From stop 2 and so on, planned again at that stop with the bikes and docks free then, and its totals read To stop 1, not counting breaks and Whole trip, not counting breaks. A later trip that can't be planned now is noted, to be planned at its stop. With stops there are no pick-up and drop-off choices and no route options. AI assistants still plan Bikeshare from a start and an end only. Rebalance Ride takes a start alone (below).

  • Systems: Metro and Metrobus[4], MARC, Light RailLink and Metro SubwayLink[5], and VRE[6].
  • On a trip leaving now, each Metro step boards the next train WMATA predicts on its line and direction that you can reach the station in time for. Metrobus steps on the chosen trip board their next predicted bus. Those departures say "live"; the time on board stays the timetable's. A step with no prediction you can catch keeps its timetable time and says "scheduled". MARC, Light RailLink and VRE are always scheduled. The plan's total reads "Door-to-door time, with live departures".
  • Live Metro data also changes which trips are offered and their order. With Own Bike + transit, a trip that takes your bike through a station WMATA reports closed is dropped, unless every trip would be. A street elevator out at a station with no working spare, or a platform elevator out, moves the trip down the list as if it took 15 minutes longer, without changing its time, and the trip shows an elevator-out icon. Elevators in a station's parking garage or lot are ignored. Live arrivals, incidents and elevator outages are shown beside the plan. An elevator outage is a warning, never a reason to skip a station.
  • Limits: walk up to 1 mi [1.6 km] to a station or 0.4 mi [0.6 km] to a bus stop; ride up to 5 mi [8 km] to a station. A trip under 1 mi [1.6 km] is offered as walking.
  • While you ride a transit trip, until you board its first train or bus, RouteMaker plans it again from where you are every 2 minutes, stopping about a minute before the departure at your stop. If another trip is now at least 3 minutes quicker, or yours can no longer be caught, the ride changes to it and says so; a delay of 3 minutes or more is said with the new arrival time. Bikeshare + transit is checked only on the walk to its first dock.
  • Trips are ranked by when you get there, with time on busy roads and waiting at junctions counting against a trip, and ranked again once the chosen trip is routed. Each trip says how far you would ride on busy roads, which is why the list is not always in strict time order.
  • Every Metro station takes a bike; steps say "by the elevator" only where an elevator is known. Metro is not used on days when bikes are not allowed on trains, such as July 4.
  • With a bike, a start, stop or end you put at a Metro or MARC station moves to the station's elevator, or where none is known its entrance, quickest to ride to from where that leg comes from, not the far side of the tracks. If that moves your point, the app says so, and Undo puts it back. An elevator you tap on the map stays where you put it.
  • Routes walk into Metro and MARC stations: the walking tunnels and the last footways between a station's entrances or elevator and the nearest road or bike path are walk-your-bike links, at most about 800 ft [250 m], and the directions say "walk your bike here", and, from the same update, escalators are closed to bikes. From the next map update, an unnamed walkway that OpenStreetMap opens to bikes (bicycle=yes or permissive, not designated), at most 500 ft [150 m] long, that reaches a rail platform in service is walked too, not ridden as a bike path, and is no longer drawn as one: at Kensington's MARC station, the ramp to the platform, the crossing over the tracks and the walkways by the station building.
  • Own Bike + transit never leaves the bike. Bikeshare + transit docks the shared bike before you board.

Walk and Walk + transit

Walk goes on foot the whole way, on sidewalks, paths and crosswalks, at 3 mph [4.8 km/h]. Walk + transit walks at each end with Metro, MARC, VRE, Light RailLink, Metro SubwayLink or a bus between, and walks the whole way where that is quickest or the trip is short. Both take a start, up to 3 stops and an end, and their walks are said turn by turn (Ride). The traffic and hills sliders are for riding, so they are not shown.

More: Walk and Walk + transit
  • Walks prefer a signal where they cross a busy road: an unsignalled crossing of a main road counts as 2 extra minutes, 4 on a highway, and a minute more with no crosswalk. An unsignalled slip lane counts a minute more. Walking along a main road with no sidewalk mapped counts more the busier the road is. A quiet street is crossed anywhere. Each crosswalk and busy crossing is said, such as "Cross Pratt Street at the signal."
  • Avoid stairs on walks, in Settings and in the settings of these two ride types and Run, keeps walks off flights of steps where a step-free way, such as a ramp or an elevator, is near. It works on Walk, Walk + transit, Run and the walks of Bikeshare and transit trips; a walk beside your own bike always avoids steps. Off unless you turn it on; kept on this device.

Run

Run goes on foot on Walk's map, with Walk's crossing rules, at your running pace: 6 mph [9.7 km/h], a 10:00 mile, unless you pick another from 4 to 10 mph [6.4 to 16 km/h] in its settings. The pace is for the flat; climbs still slow it. It is kept on this device and never put in a link. Run has no traffic slider; its hills slider runs from the middle, not minding hills, to the left, keeping off climbs where a flatter way is near. Avoid stairs on walks works on Run too. It takes stops and Make it a loop, and is followed turn by turn on foot, as a walk is; its button reads Start run.

More: Run
  • Among the ways the router finds, a second on a trail, park path or track (packed gravel and dirt are fine) counts as 0.7 of a second on a busier street, and a second on a quiet street as 0.85. Time in an unbroken stretch of trail or quiet street at least 0.5 mi [800 m] long counts 10% less again; a stretch ends where it crosses or runs along a busier road, or crosses at a traffic light. Each traffic light counts 15 seconds of waiting three times, as Road Bike's waits do, so a run through fewer lights wins.
  • After dark, from civil dusk to civil dawn or with the Night ride time, and on a run expected to finish after dusk, an unlit way counts about twice its time, and a way not mapped as lit counts as unlit, so the run leans to lit ways without refusing the rest.
  • A loop runs the way back off the way out where another way exists, as a ride's loop does.
  • Target distance: with only the start placed and Make it a loop on, the run tries a loop in each of six directions from your start, sized to the target, each from how much longer than its straight sides the ones before it ran. It keeps one within about 12% of the target, preferring one that runs no way twice, then the one with the most trail and fewest lights for its length; where none is that near, it takes the nearest and sizes it again from what it ran, up to three times. The target can be up to 31 mi [50 km]. With stops placed, it works as Trailmaxxing's: each part of the run gets its share, and the way with the most trail and fewest traffic lights at or under it is taken.

How much a busy road costs

RouteMaker compares routes in calm miles: a mile of quiet street counts as 1, and a busier mile counts as more, as if it were longer. The traffic slider and the ride type together decide how much more. Routing has the cost of each level on every ride type, and everything else a route pays for.

More: How much a busy road costs

Above 80 on the slider, RouteMaker's own search also weighs each route's exposure to busy roads. Ride types differ in how heavily they count LTS 4 and Avoid:

Transportation ride types

For getting somewhere: Default for everyday riding, Novice and Nervous for riders new to traffic, and E-bike for assisted riding and Cargo Bike for a long, heavy bike. Bikeshare and Bike + transit have their own sections.

More: Transportation ride types

Default

Everyday riding that prefers paths and quiet streets, even when a busier road would be faster. The slider starts at 70: a mile of LTS 3 costs about 3 to 6 calm miles and a mile of LTS 4 about 13 to 16. Traffic-free paths and protected bike lanes count for a little less than a quiet street, so routes lean toward them. With Easy MTB trails on (it starts off), routes may also use mountain-bike trails rated Green (0 or 1) and unrated ones, timed at no more than 6.2 mph [10 km/h] there; see Easy MTB trails.

Novice and Nervous

For riders new to traffic. Every road and junction is read one level busier than it is, so LTS 3 is treated as LTS 4, and busy roads are avoided hard. Detours are kept shorter than on Trailmaxxing (at most 1.4 times the length of the router's quickest route). Where a route still has to use real LTS 3 or worse, or the detour limit is reached, RouteMaker offers transit and bikeshare instead. Where a route still rides a road rated LTS 4 or worse for up to about 0.5 mi [800 m] and a sidewalk or path runs beside it, the directions say to walk your bike on the sidewalk there, the ride says so ahead, the walk is drawn on the map, and the route's time counts it at walking pace. A road with no sidewalk is still ridden. Novice and Nervous's Own Bike + transit and Bikeshare + transit offers also try riding to a station or stop near where the busiest stretch begins, taking the train or bus past it and riding on; that trip's row says "skips the busy stretch". Grades past 5%, uphill or downhill, are marked on the map and avoided where they can be, as on Mass Ride (Steep grades).

E-bike

Leaves out ways mapped as closed to e-bikes, starts the Hills slider at 12%, so a more direct route over a hill wins where it is quicker, and times trips at an assisted 15 mph [24 km/h].

Cargo Bike

A long, heavy bike. Gentle grades, few gates or barriers (each counts about 15 minutes when choosing a route, though only 30 seconds of it shows in the time; from the next map update a plain, open gate not mapped narrower than 4.9 ft [1.5 m] costs nothing), and stronger avoidance of rough surfaces. Choose carrying cargo or with passengers; passengers start the slider at 80 and count LTS 2 streets a little, so routes lean toward LTS 1. Electric assist switches to the e-bike map and a faster pace, but hills still count: a loaded cargo bike's motor rarely makes a climb easy. With a strong motor, move the hills slider toward Fastest yourself.

Bikeshare

More: Bikeshare

Bikeshare

Capital Bikeshare only. Choose a classic bike (slower, avoids hills) or an e-bike. Walking speed is 3 mph [4.8 km/h]. Stations that have not reported for 30 minutes are ignored. A dock that adds less than 165 ft [50 m] of riding counts as "on your way". The station lists always show the three best stations, with e-bike counts.

Rebalance Ride

Moves a bikeshare bike from a crowded station to one that needs bikes. Rides like this can earn reward points (Bike Angels in the Capital Bikeshare app); the points show there, not in RouteMaker.

It takes a start only, with no destination, stops or route options. The plan walks to the crowded dock (at least three quarters full) with the quickest walk among the 3 nearest, which are its 3 pick-up options, and the station list shows pick-up stations only. Its end is a guess: as you reach the dock, Ride holds and asks Where to dock?, with up to 3 docks that need bikes (at most a quarter full) from the live counts, nearest first. The ride goes to the one you pick and ends by itself there; if the page reloads on the way, Resume rides on to that dock. With no list (no signal, or none needing bikes), Try again or ride to the plan's own dock. The end screen shows Today's Rebalance Rides: trips, walking, riding and total time since midnight, kept on this device only. Do another trip plans the next from where you are, and Finish session ends.

Adventure ride types

For riding for its own sake: Fast for direct roads, Road Bike for pavement, Tandem for a bike built for two, Trailmaxxing for the calmest route, Gravel, Mountain Goat and Mountain Bike off the pavement.

More: Adventure ride types

Fast

Direct roads, few turns and smooth pavement, whatever the traffic. The slider starts at 10, so a mile of LTS 4 costs only about 2 calm miles. Where the route rides 0.25 mi [400 m] or more of winding trail (our default), that trail counts as an LTS 3 street, as on Road Bike. A winding trail bends through a total of 40 degrees or more per 330 ft [100 m]. The route is then planned once more without it, and the better of the two is kept.

Road Bike

For a road bike: keeps off gravel, dirt and winding trails where there is a paved way round, makes fewer turns, and times the ride at a road bike's pace, 15.5 mph [25 km/h]. It plans on its own map, where gravel, fine gravel and compacted surfaces cost a road bike about seventeen times a paved path, and a trail that bends through a total of 40 degrees or more per 330 ft [100 m], such as Sligo Creek Trail's bending stretches, costs as much as an LTS 3 street. Each change of street costs 30 seconds, as on Fast and Group Ride. When it chooses between routes, a wait at a junction, such as a signal or stop sign, counts three times over, at most five times the wait at a red (the owner's choice), and a crossing that may need a button press for the light counts as 10 minutes, so a route with fewer lights can win; the time shown is still the real wait. From the next map update, at the weekend it rides Beach Drive and the other parkways closed to cars as paths; when that weekend map is down, Plan says "There is no weekend road bike map right now, so this route counts parkways like Beach Drive as roads with cars." The traffic slider starts at 40, Balanced, and the hills slider in the middle. Its search for calm trails leaves winding trails out too. With "Keep to roads, not trails" on it plans on the roads-only map, where gravel counts as compacted gravel, as on other ride types. It replaces the Avoid gravel box: an older link with that box ticked on Default, Fast, Gravel or Mountain Goat opens as Road Bike. On Novice and Nervous or Trailmaxxing it keeps its own ride type and drops the box.

Tandem

For a bicycle built for two. A tandem is long and cannot thread a narrow gap, so a gate, cycle barrier or narrow bollard counts about 15 minutes when RouteMaker chooses a route, though only 30 seconds of it shows in the time, and its routes keep out of them where there is another way. From the next map update a plain, open gate not mapped narrower than 4.9 ft [1.5 m] costs nothing, so a tandem route can use a trail with one. Each change of street costs 30 seconds, as on Road Bike, because a long bike swings wide at every corner. Two riders are quick on the flat and slow up a climb, so the hills slider starts at 5%, as on Group Ride, and descents steeper than 5% cost too, with two riders' weight on one set of brakes. Rough surfaces cost as much as on Cargo Bike. The rider behind does not steer, so the traffic slider starts at 80, as for Cargo Bike with passengers, and LTS 2 streets count a little, so routes lean toward LTS 1. When it chooses between routes, a wait at a junction counts twice, because both riders stop and start together, and a crossing that may need a button press for the light counts as 10 minutes, as on Road Bike; the time shown is still the real wait. Times are at a tandem's pace, 13 mph [21 km/h] on the flat.

Trailmaxxing

The calmest route, aimed at your target distance: paths, protected lanes and quiet streets, with busy streets only where there is no other way. It finds the least stressful route, not the most trail, so it may add miles. With a target distance it aims at or under it and goes over only where the extra miles buy a real cut in stress, never past 1.25 times the target. With no target it stays within 1.6 times the router's quickest route (and at least 1 mi [1.6 km] more). Easy MTB trails, off at first, lets it use Green and unrated mountain-bike trails too, as on Default.

Gravel

Unpaved roads and trails cost nothing extra. It does not yet seek them out.

Mountain Goat

Looks for climbs: picks the hilliest of a few alternative routes, up to half again as long. Climbs are categorised by length times grade, from Category 5 to Hors Catégorie[9].

Mountain Bike

Seeks mountain-bike trails up to the level you choose (1 to 4, default 2; unrated trails count as 2), joined by calm roads: LTS 2 freely, LTS 3 only for gaps under 0.5 mi [0.8 km]. A trail mile counts as half a road mile, or 0.4 at your preferred level. It plans a loop from your start unless you turn the loop off, never uses trails closed to bikes or mapped closed when wet, and links to MORE's trail conditions[7].

Mountain Bike, Mountain Goat and Gravel each work differently from the road ride types: Mountain Bike, Mountain Goat and Gravel explains how.

Group ride types

For riding together: Group Ride for a group riding with traffic, and Mass Ride for a large group that takes the roadway.

More: Group ride types

Group Ride

A group riding with traffic at riding pace: quiet roads, fewer turns (each change of street costs 30 seconds, against 5 on Default). Winding trails count as an LTS 3 street, as on Fast (see Fast for the 0.25 mi [400 m] minimum and the one re-plan).

Mass Ride

A large group taking the roadway, such as a bike party: roads only, no trails, no bike-lane preference, never against a one-way street's traffic, few turns, at a parade pace of 6 mph [9.7 km/h]. No loops. The slider is locked at 0, because a group of hundreds controls its lane. Its map shows how many riders a minute each road can carry instead of traffic stress, for a ride of 100 to 2,000 riders[8], along with notes on federal land and reviewed hazard reports. It works inside DC only, as DC is the only place with measured lane widths to work out that capacity from; for a point outside DC, the app offers Group Ride with trails off. Its method is unlike any other ride type's: Mass Ride explains it.

Options for any ride type

Two sliders and a few switches work on any ride type: how much traffic you accept, how you feel about hills, and whether to keep to roads.

More: Options for any ride type
  • Traffic slider: named notches from Traffic tolerant (10) through Direct (25), Balanced (40), Prefers quiet (70), Low-stress (80) and Calm (90) to Calmest (100).
  • Hills slider: the steepest grade you can climb for several minutes in your lowest gears without labored breathing, 1% to 15%. Every climb counts as the time it takes, and grades past yours count for more. Mountain Goat alone keeps its right half to seek climbs. A route never gets busier just because you set a lower grade.
  • Keep to roads, not trails uses the roads-only map.
  • Weekend rides treat roads closed to cars at weekends, such as Beach Drive, as paths. The weekend is Saturday and Sunday by day, until 6 PM on Sunday; a weekday federal holiday plans as Weekday off-hours. Road Bike does this on its own map from the next map update. A weekend Gravel, Mountain Goat, Mountain Bike, Easy MTB trails or e-bike ride near such a road is planned both ways, and the better route is kept.
  • Go's route choice draws up to two more routes beside yours, on ride types other than transit, Rebalance Ride and Mass Ride: More direct, up to two notches more tolerant of traffic, shown only when it saves at least a minute, and Trailmaxxed, planned at the top of the traffic slider, shown only when it takes at least 250 ft [80 m] off busy riding. Press a line or its row to ride it; your own setting stays as it was.
