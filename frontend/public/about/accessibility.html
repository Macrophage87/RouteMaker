Accessibility

RouteMaker is built to be used without seeing the map: with a screen reader, a keyboard, large text or high contrast. This page lists every accessibility setting, where it is, and how to use it.

At a glance

  • All the settings are on the Settings page: in Plan, the last button in the bar at the bottom of the planner; in Go, the Menu.
  • High contrast for stronger lines, text and colors; Accessibility mode for keyboard and screen reader map tools; Announcements for how much a screen reader is told.
  • For rides: Say cues with, How much to say and Large text during a ride.
  • Every setting is kept on the device you set it on.

Looking for something else? What the stress levels mean: Stress ratings. Turn cues and leaving the route: While you ride.

Where the settings are

Open Settings: in Plan it is the last button in the bar at the bottom of the planner, and in Go it is the Menu. The riding settings are under Riding and the rest under Display. Each is kept on the device you set it on, not in your account, so set them once on each phone or computer you use[1].

Seeing the map

High contrast makes the map and the panels stronger, and the stress map never relies on color alone: each level has its own dash pattern and width.

More: Seeing the map
  • High contrast (Settings, Display) draws the stress lines and their outlines bolder, makes the panel's borders and grey text stronger, darkens the street names on the map, and switches the stress map to colors that don't rely on red and green. The dash patterns stay the same. It turns on by itself when your device asks for more contrast[2] and you haven't chosen, and then says so; you can turn it off there. Map layers says where it is.
  • Fonts. The app is set in Atkinson Hyperlegible, and the street names on the map in Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, typefaces designed by the Braille Institute for low-vision readers[3]. Street names sit on a white outline so they stay readable over the colored lines. Major trails are named along the line in purple, from the zoom where every street appears: a trail on a regional or longer bike route (or, if paved, a long walking route), or a named trail at least 2.5 mi [4 km] long (5 mi [8 km] if unpaved). Metro and MARC station names are drawn larger than street names (18 px, 24 px while riding) and on top of the route and other map marks, from city zoom in. Where names crowd, the station with the most lines keeps its name. Town names are in extra-bold type, and neighborhood names in extra-bold small capitals, both on the same white outline.
  • Text size. Panels follow your browser's or phone's zoom. During a ride, the Large text button (Aa) makes the next turn as big as it fits on the top half of the screen, with no ride text under 16 pt, and makes the map's street names larger.
  • Dark mode follows your device for the panels; the map stays light, so the stress colors read the same. Windows' contrast themes are supported[2].
  • Less motion. When your device asks for reduced motion, the turning section arrows and the sliding of points as you reorder them are turned off.

With a screen reader or keyboard

Everything you can do on the map can also be done without it. Turn on Accessibility mode for Map tools, choose how much you are told with Announcements, and use the directions and junction lists to read a route.

More: With a screen reader or keyboard

Getting started

  1. Press Tab once: the first button on the page is Accessibility mode. Turn it on, and focus moves to Map tools, by the map's zoom buttons. It is also in Settings, Display. With it on, Plan's panel says how to use Map tools.
  2. Press Tab again for Skip to the route planner (Skip to ride mode during a ride). The planner is a landmark named Route planner.
  3. Type places into From, To and Add a stop in the planner. Stops can be put in another order with the keyboard, or removed, there too, without the map.

Map tools

With Accessibility mode on, Map tools by the zoom buttons offers Add point at map center (in Go, Set destination at map center) and Road info at map center. Move the map with the arrow keys, then choose one. Escape closes Map tools and returns focus to its button. The keys above work whether Accessibility mode is on or off; with NVDA or JAWS, the map's keys need focus mode.

Announcements

Announcements in Settings, Display, sets what screen readers are told. Brief says the route, guidance and errors. Full also says what the app is doing, such as "Finding your location" and "Planning a route". If you leave it unset, Go is brief and Plan is full. Routes, guidance and errors are always said.

Reading a route

  • Directions is the route as a numbered list, with each road's stress level and each warning in words. Full detail adds more, and the list can be copied or downloaded as text.
  • The junction list in the route summary names each stressful junction and how far along it is; each row opens the same card as the marker on the map.
  • Road info says a road's name, level and why, in words: street names on the map itself are pictures a screen reader can't reach.
  • Manual mode in Plan says when it turns on and off, and a label under the map's bar says it is on.

During a ride

Choose who says the cues and how much they say. The first Start ride in Plan asks both (Go never asks); you can change them later in Settings, Riding, or in Ride settings during a ride.

More: During a ride
  • Say cues with: My screen reader, RouteMaker's voice, Both, or Neither (on screen only). Cues never move focus.
  • How much to say: Full, every turn, early and again close to it; Stoker, each turn once ahead and as it happens, with busy crossings and stops, made for a blind stoker on a tandem; Quiet, only arrival.
  • Where am I? says where you are at any time and centers the map on you again; on a keyboard, press W or C while focus is in the ride panel.
  • Cues say when the route goes onto a service road, alley or parking aisle, and name the main road beside it: "Left onto the service road beside East-West Highway."
  • At a traffic circle the cue counts the exits: "Take the 3rd exit onto P Street NW at Dupont Circle". Only streets are counted, not slip lanes, service lanes or paths. Where the turn card draws an unusual junction as a picture, the cue says the same as it would with a plain arrow.
  • The Large text button (T on a keyboard, in the ride panel) turns large text on and off; a screen reader hears whether it is pressed, and each press says "Large text on" or "off".
  • With Say cues with set to My screen reader, the map is hidden from the screen reader during a ride, so swiping goes from the next turn straight to the ride's buttons.
  • Turn the map to my heading, in Ride settings, turns the map with you; turn it off to keep north up.
  • In a ride the map's street names are larger and shortened (R St NW), and the turn card uses fewer words (0.2 mi, not In 0.2 mi; 20 min, not about 20 min) so its type and arrow can be larger. A screen reader still hears the whole sentence, including "about".
  • End ride is a slider, so it isn't ended by a stray tap; the arrow keys or End move it too.
  • End ride and Road closed have larger text, and stay on one line on the narrowest phones.
  • When a ride ends, "Ride ended." is said and the focus goes to Done; the figures follow on the ride summary card. The elevation chart and the stress bar each have a text version beside them. The summary stays until you choose Done or Undo. It is always in large text, one figure to a line, with nothing smaller than 16 pt.

In the Android app

The Android app keeps cues going with the screen locked[4].

More: In the Android app
  • Cues are said with the phone's own text-to-speech, and location keeps working with the screen locked, shown by a "Riding with RouteMaker" notification. It never asks for location all the time.
  • The latest cue is shown in a silent notification, so TalkBack doesn't say each cue twice.
  • A short buzz confirms when a long press picks up the route line, and Group Ride alerts buzz and are said at once.
  • In a browser, RouteMaker asks to keep the screen on during a ride and says so if it can't.

What RouteMaker aims for

The aim is WCAG 2.2 level AA[5] for everything outside the map itself, with nothing shown by color alone and a non-map way to do every action. Screen reader users and blind stokers on tandems are part of the intended audience, and each release is checked in a real browser for focus order, contrast and screen reader names.
