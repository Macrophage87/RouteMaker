Using RouteMaker

How to use RouteMaker, screen by screen. Each section starts with the short version; open More for the detail.

At a glance

  • Go, for a quick trip: say where, choose a line, Start ride.
  • Plan, for building a route: points, ride type, directions.
  • Both show the same route; the switch at the top changes between them.
  • Layers is on Go's top bar, and Save and load is in Go's Menu. In Plan, both are on the bottom bar.

Looking for something else? How routes are chosen: Routing. The ride types: Ride types. Screen reader and keyboard use: Accessibility.

Go

Type where you are going in Where to?, or press a place button, then choose one of the lines on the map and press Start ride[1].

More: Go
  • Where to? starts finding the route while you are still looking at the top result. Tapping the empty box lists the places you picked recently, once there are some.
  • Place buttons: Home, Food, Water and Restroom; More adds Metro (the three nearest stations, closed ones marked), Repair, Sights, Featured routes and Change home. The first press of Home asks where home is: search for it or tap the map.
  • The route card shows the start, the number of stops and the destination; Edit opens the points. Add a stop under it puts a place where it adds the least distance, and Best order appears with two or more stops. Undo takes a stop back out.
  • Route choice: beside your route, Go can draw More direct and Trailmaxxed, each with how much time or busy riding it saves or adds. More direct is up to two notches more tolerant of traffic and is shown only when it saves at least a minute. Trailmaxxed is planned at the top of the traffic slider and is shown only when it takes at least 250 ft [80 m] off busy riding. Press a line, or its row, to choose it; your own setting doesn't change.
  • Transit offer: on Novice and Nervous, when the only way through is a busy road, Own bike + transit and Bikeshare + transit appear beside the route, each naming the busy stretch it avoids.
  • Ride types: Go shows the commuting types as buttons; More ride types lists every type in groups, with a search box. A star puts a type you use first, and is kept with your account when you are signed in.
  • Shared places: a location shared from another app (a map pin or a link) opens in RouteMaker as the destination.
  • Featured routes, behind More, opens the list of featured routes: rides written up around one exact route, those in season first, and those out of season in a closed Out of season fold. Each is a page of its own you can share, with, where the post has them, the title and its season, a row of figures (distance, climbing, descent, riding time and ride type), the map, Open in RouteMaker, Download GPX, Send to Garmin and Send to Wahoo, Plan's elevation and traffic stress charts, then About this ride, Who it's for and Highlights; a highlight whose stretch the post marks has a small map of it. The route is a copy kept as it was when the post was written, so a map update or an edit of the saved route does not change it. Only the site's admins write them. A link to a post shows its map as the picture once an admin has set it, and the route's line until then. A post can carry up to 20 photos, under About this ride or under a highlight, each with alt text and sometimes a caption. An admin adds them on the post's page, under For admins, Photos: alt text saying what the photo shows is required, the caption is optional, and Where picks the section. The browser makes the photo smaller and saves it again as a JPEG, so its location, date and camera details don't go with it; the site removes anything that's left. If the browser can't open a photo (some phone formats), save it as a JPEG or PNG first. To change a photo's alt text, caption, section or order, or to delete it, use the admin. If its highlight is removed, the photo moves under About this ride.

Roam

With nothing planned in Go (no start or destination), on any ride type but Mass Ride, the big button is Roam: a ride with nowhere set.

  • The map follows you, and the card names the road you are on and, once you are moving, up to three roads ahead, within about half a mile [800 m], each with how far it is in a straight line and its stress level. They are read from the map already on your phone, so nothing is sent.
  • Detour has four buttons, Water, Restroom, Food and Repair (repair stands and bike shops), each listing the three nearest by bike, and a Search box for anything else. What you type there, at least 3 letters, is matched against Find food's places to eat by cuisine or name ("pizza", "coffee"), and is also sent to the place search, as Where to? does, for any name, shop or address ("grocery", "bike shop"), with your position rounded to a grid about 0.6 mile [1 km] across. The matches from both are measured by bike together and the three nearest are listed. A word for one of the buttons ("bathroom", "pump", "lunch") does that button's search instead. Ride here starts an ordinary ride there, and ending that ride brings you back to roaming.
  • Weather where you are is checked every 5 minutes, for alerts and for rain in the next hour ("Rain expected in about 45 min, where you are."), said and shown as in a ride.
  • Broadcast, a round button, shares a link others can follow your dot and the way you ride with, while your app is open (see ride links).
  • The next sunrise and sunset show in the corner, with the minutes left once it is within the hour.
  • End roam is slid, like End ride, so a bump does not end it.
  • Your position stays on your phone, except when you search for a Detour or ride to one (a typed search also sends your words, with the rounded position, to the place search), for the weather check every 5 minutes (rounded on the server to about 0.7 mi [1.1 km] before anyone else is asked), and while you broadcast, when the server keeps it for 2 minutes.

Plan

Plan is the full planner: build the route point by point, choose how it should ride, and read its directions, elevation and junctions before you go.

More: Plan

Points

  • How to start: Help, the (i) button by the map's credits on every screen, opens a short how-to, the four-line summary of how RouteMaker works, links to these pages, and the map's credits. It starts closed. With no points yet, Start at current location (in the Route button's place) sets your start where you are.
  • Type places into From, To and Add a stop, or tap the map. Up to 25 points in all.
  • Once there is a start and an end, a tap or click on the map adds a via point (in a loop, a tap adds a stop), a small hollow ring that only shapes the route: it has no number and is never a cue on the ride. Ctrl+click (Cmd+click on a Mac), or Add as stop in the road panel, adds a stop, a numbered blue pin, and asks for a name, which you can leave blank. Add as stop on a food, water, sight or station card names the stop after the place without asking, and a place you pick in Add a stop is a stop too. A stop's menu has Name this stop (Rename stop once it has one), and the menu of any point between the start and the end turns a via point into a stop or back.
  • A stop's name, the one you gave it or the place's when you added it from a search, a card or a list, goes with it: in links and saved routes, in GPX route points, in FIT and TCX course points, to Garmin and Wahoo, and in the ride's cues ("You have reached stop 2, Union Market.").
  • Drag a stop's grip to move it, or use Best order to put the stops in the order that rides best; the start and the finish stay where they are.
  • On most ride types, Plan starts as a loop: the first point is the start and finish, each later tap is a stop, and the ride comes back. Untick Loop for a ride that ends somewhere else, or right-click the map (press and hold on a phone) and choose Set as end, choose Make this the destination on a search result, or End here on a station's card. Each ends the ride there and turns the loop off. Reverse rides the stops the other way round.
  • While you plan, bold lines with arrows join your points in the order you'll ride them. A loop also shows its way back to the start. Drag a line to add a via point in that leg, the same as on a planned route.
  • Undo and Redo cover every change to the points (Ctrl+Z, and Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y). More has Reset and Clear.
  • Manual mode draws straight lines between points you place, for a stretch the router won't take; turning it on during a loop turns the loop off. See Routing.

How it rides

  • Ride type and its traffic and hills sliders: Ride types.
  • Ride time: Weekend, Weekday rush, Weekday off-hours, or Night, 9 PM to 7 AM. Rush hour and night change how busy roads are taken to be; weekends open roads closed to cars at weekends.
  • Cargo Bike asks what you carry: Carrying cargo, or Cargo with passengers.
  • Rider and bike weight (Settings, Riding) makes climbs cost what they cost you; it is kept private.
  • Show bike lanes on high-stress roads (Layers, Traffic stress) draws the painted lanes on LTS 4 and Avoid roads and counts them in the route's totals. Off unless you turn it on.
  • When you are not signed in, a plan whose points are more than about 93 mi [150 km] apart asks you to confirm before it is planned.

Reading the route

Under the route are its folds: Elevation and climbs, Stress and facilities, Directions (with Full detail, and a copy or download as text), Junctions to watch, Other routes (up to three more at the top of the traffic slider) and Water and restrooms along the way. Each starts closed, and opening one of them closes the one that was open. A line under the time gives sunrise or sunset when it falls during the ride or within about an hour of arriving, worked out on your device[2]. On a screen at least 1200 px wide, the route's results (its totals, Start ride, GPX, Link and these folds) sit in their own card on the right of the map, and the planner on the left keeps the points, the ride type, its settings and the Route button. On the map, the route line carries the name of each street or path it follows, in Plan, Go and rides.

Weather. In Go and Plan, the route's warnings include any National Weather Service alert in force along it while you would ride it, leaving now (a plan set for another time is not checked), each with an NWS notice link to the NWS's page for the place, and a rain line such as "Rain expected in about 20 min, near mile 5.0 (8.0 km)." with Forecast: Open-Meteo beside it. Start ride never waits for this. See Weather for which alerts are shown.

On the ride screen

Start ride follows you along the route with turn cues, re-plans if you leave it, and keeps the map for the route in case you lose signal. Start ride is made for a phone on your bike, so on a computer it first asks (This looks like a computer): Go back, or Start anyway, which isn't asked again in that tab.

More: On the ride screen
  • Start ride starts at once, in Plan as in Go, with the cues set in Settings, Riding (RouteMaker's voice, saying every turn, until you change them); see Accessibility. The screen is kept on while you ride.
  • At an unusual junction, such as a five-way or a left onto a service road, the turn card draws the junction instead of a plain arrow. Your way is solid with its arrowhead, the other roads are dashed, and a service road's main road is drawn where it lies: behind the turn when you cross it first, ahead when you turn short of it. At a traffic circle the card draws the ring with your exit's number in it, and the cue counts the exits, for example "Take the 3rd exit onto P Street NW at Dupont Circle". Only streets are counted, not slip lanes, service lanes or paths. Ordinary crossroads keep the plain arrow.
  • A short path across a road between two turns is said as the crossing, with a straight-on arrow: "Cross Redland Road, then left onto Redland Road bike lane". A trail fork whose branches both lean off (a Y) gets its own cue, such as "Bear left to stay on Rock Creek Trail", and a hairpin, such as a ramp curling up onto a bridge, is said on the side you actually turn.
  • To go: under the turn, a line gives the distance and time to the next stop and to the end, and the clock time you should get there, in your phone's 12- or 24-hour clock: "To stop 2: 1.2 mi (1.9 km), 8 min · 6:42 AM". A screen reader hears "about 8 min, arriving about 6:42 AM". The times start from the route's own time (How a route's time is worked out). Once you have ridden half a mile [800 m] they follow your own pace against the plan, wholly by 3.1 mi [5 km], and never more than twice as fast or as slow. A halt counts for up to 90 seconds, so a long stop doesn't count against you, and time off the route, paused or walking is left out.
  • Where am I? says where you are and centers the map on you again. It also says when you should reach the next stop and the end: ", arriving about 6:42 AM". Large text makes the next turn as big as it fits.
  • Broadcast, where your device has a share sheet, sends a Share ride link, as Broadcast beside Start does, and once it has gone makes you the ride's leader; your ride carries on. It is not on Ride the rest, a Roam detour, a ride you opened from someone else's link, or once you have arrived.
  • Detour has four buttons, Water, Restroom, Food and Repair (repair stands and bike shops), each listing the three nearest by bike, and a Search box for anything else. What you type there, at least 3 letters, is matched against Find food's places to eat by cuisine or name ("pizza", "coffee"), and is also sent to the place search, as Where to? does, for any name, shop or address ("grocery", "bike shop"), with your position rounded to a grid about 0.6 mile [1 km] across. The matches from both are measured by bike together and the three nearest are listed. A word for one of the buttons ("bathroom", "pump", "lunch") does that button's search instead. Detour here on a place it lists re-plans the ride through it and on along the rest of the route. With a stop ahead, except on a shared or fixed route, each place it lists has Instead of stop 2 (with that stop's number) to go there in its place, and a screen reader hears the button as Detour with "or change stop 2" at the end; Skip this stop is in More.
  • Road closed plans a way round from where you are: "Road closed. Finding a way round."
  • 311 logs a problem where you are, for reporting later; see below.
  • More opens, under the top row, Change the route: Less traffic (a car with a down arrow) and Fewer hills (a mountain with a down arrow) plan again from where you are, with the way ahead first. Less traffic moves the ride's traffic slider one step toward Calmest, Fewer hills its hills slider one step toward avoiding hills. If the new route keeps to your line, you hear "Same route: no calmer way from here." (or "no less steep way"). Press again for the next step; at the last one you hear "Already the calmest route." or "Already the flattest route." (On a Mass Ride, whose traffic level is set, Less traffic always says so.) The steps last for this ride only, Plan's sliders stay as they were, and a step that finds no route, or only one leading back to the old line, is undone. With a stop ahead, a line names it (such as "Next stop: Stop 2, Union Market") above Skip this stop. These are hidden on a shared or fixed route and once you have arrived, and pressing one closes the panel. Below them are the ride's settings: who says the cues and how much, and Turn the map to my heading. Escape also closes the panel. A screen reader hears each in full, such as "Less traffic: plan again from here", and the button as "More: change the route, and ride settings".
  • The stops still ahead are drawn on the ride's map with their numbers and names; via points are not.
  • Already done? If the ride finds you already past one of its stops, as when you start again part way along or resume a ride, it lists the route's stops with the ones behind you ticked ("Ticked stops are skipped. Untick one to go back to it."). Continue rides the rest. Untick a stop and the ride plans a way back to it; tick one ahead to skip it. Escape is Continue with the ticks as they are. It is not asked after Undo on the ride summary or on a Bikeshare or transit leg.
  • Resume your ride? Start ride keeps the ride's route and stops on this device only, never on the server: no track, times or distances, only what was planned. Each new plan during the ride replaces it, and End ride removes it. If the app closes during a ride, opening it again within 12 hours of the route's last change offers Resume ride, which starts the ride again from where you are, or Not now, which forgets it; Escape only puts the question off until the app next opens. Roam, Ride the rest, a Roam detour and Bikeshare or transit legs cannot be resumed yet.
  • Weather is checked once the ride knows where you are on the route, and every 5 minutes, along the rest of the route. A new alert is said once ("Weather alert: Severe Thunderstorm Warning until 6:45 PM.", with "until" only where the NWS gives the end time), and again only if the NWS changes its end time or severity, interrupting for an Extreme alert or a Severe warning; rain is said when it is first forecast, when it comes within 15 minutes, and when it turns heavy. Both stay in the bottom panel until you press the big Dismiss button (an X) beside them; an alert you have not dismissed, or rain coming within 15 minutes or turning heavy, brings them back.
  • Left alone. During a ride or Roam, if you have moved the map by hand, 30 seconds with no touch, wheel or key and nothing open puts it back on you, following at the ride's zoom (Where am I? does it at once). The same 30 seconds closes a map card (a stop, place, station, closure or climb), the road info and the side menus (Detour, More, the dock, 311, Riders, Alert), and the map then goes back to you. Kept open: a menu you are typing or speaking in (a Detour search, Alert's Other, its microphone) and anything asking you something (Alert's Send to everyone?, Already done?, a stop's name, the group's question, Resume your ride?). In accessibility mode nothing closes on its own. A map card's close button (X) is 44 px across.
  • Off the route, RouteMaker plans again from where you are. Whenever the ride plans again (off the route, Re-plan from here, Road closed, Less traffic, Fewer hills or a Detour), it keeps every stop still ahead but treats via points as guides: one less than half a mile [800 m] ahead is dropped, so you are never sent back to touch it. Keep the planned route stops that until you are back on it, and shows the way back instead.
  • No signal: at Start ride, the map within about 1,000 ft [300 m] either side of the route is kept on your device, so dead spots still show the stress lines. It is cleared when the ride ends. Only map tiles are kept, never where you were.
  • In the background: after 10 minutes with the page out of sight, the ride pauses to save battery and stops using your location; Resume carries on.
  • Back gesture: swiping back during a ride does nothing, so a glove or a bump can't leave the ride.
  • Bikeshare rides show the time since you undocked, counting up, and Ride to the nearest dock finds the closest dock with room, or the one that adds least to the ride. Stopping by a dock for a while marks the bike as docked; Restart the time starts the count again.
  • Waiting for a train or bus, the ride shows where the next one is and when it is due. Times say whether they are live or scheduled, and a countdown shows when there are under five minutes to spare.
  • End ride shows the ride summary; Undo picks the ride back up, keeping the figures recorded so far. See While you ride.

Layers and the road panel

Layers turns the map's extras on and off, one switch per section, with each section's key inside it. Pressing and holding a road, or right-clicking it, opens the road panel.

More: Layers and the road panel

The layers

  • Traffic stress: the stress map, Show bike lanes on high-stress roads, the junction markers and the route's hazard marks. The levels: Stress ratings. At zoom 11 and further out, the stress map draws only the long trails, such as the Capital Crescent, the Metropolitan Branch Trail and the W&OD, and roads closed to cars at set times. They show from the map's widest zoom, drawn above its main roads. The busiest bike corridors and long calm streets show there too, and the long trails are named from zoom 10; from the next map update the well-known corridors are named at zoom 10 and 11 too. From zoom 12 the other paths and roads join them, with busy roads drawn faint until zoom 14. In a city the map builds up as a road map does for cars. Off-road paths, protected lanes, the busier popular bike corridors and long calm streets come first, at zoom 12. Painted lanes and the other corridors follow at zoom 13. Every other street, sidewalk and busy road waits for zoom 14, and the base map still shows the streets until then. From the next map update the network also joins up. At zoom 10 and 11 the calmest streets between the trails, and the minor trails that join them, show with the long trails, and stray bits under a mile long that join nothing are left off at zoom 11 and below. From zoom 12 a short street or two (up to about 650 ft [200 m]) linking two bike lanes or paths, a calm street joining a short bit of lane or path to the rest, and a single busy block (up to about 820 ft [250 m]) in a street drawn at zoom 12 or 13 show from zoom 12, while a short bit that joins nothing and a dead end such as a cul-de-sac wait for zoom 14. On- and off-ramps wait for zoom 14 everywhere unless they have a bike lane or path or are a popular corridor. Where lines cross, the calmer way is drawn on top.
  • Water and restrooms: drinking fountains and taps (with untreated water marked), flush and portable restrooms, and bike repair stands, from street zoom. Where they would crowd each other, as downtown, some wait until you zoom in. The route's list names all the water and restrooms near your route, and Repair, in Go's More, lists the nearest stands.
  • Rail stations: Metro and MARC, with closed stations struck through and elevator outages on the station icons. A MARC station's card gives the next scheduled train each way, and from the next timetable update names its line, such as Penn Line.
  • Bikeshare and e-bikes: Capital Bikeshare docks, with the bikes, e-bikes and room at each.
  • Places to see: museums, gardens, viewpoints and more. Off unless you turn it on.
  • Trails and terrain: trail ratings, climbs and topo lines; see Mountain Bike, Mountain Goat and Gravel.
  • In Plan, also Scenic and DC permits, both off by default. Neither changes the route.
  • Closures in force now show on the map from zoom 11 even with no route (a route's own closures show at any zoom): red where a road or path is closed, orange where a bike lane is blocked or construction can be ridden past. Each leaves the map once it ends. DDOT's permits show only with DC permits on. More: Closures.

Finding things on the map

  • Plan's map has a Find button that opens Food, Water and Places to see. Find food has Crab, Coffee, Pizza, Tacos, Ice cream and Farm stands, near you or in the map's view.
  • Repair stands and bike shops (Go's More, Repair) lists the nearest.
  • On towpaths and long trails, trail mile markers are shown on the map, in the road panel and in Where am I?.

The road panel

  • The road's name, its stress level and why, in words.
  • Use this spot: Set as start, Add as stop (it asks for a name) or Set as end, or Set as Home.
  • Street View, the newest Mapillary photo facing along the road where there is one, and Edit in OSM to fix the map itself.
  • Suggest a change…: a road's level, a junction, or a hazard on a Mass Ride.

Save, share and export

Save and load, on Plan's bottom bar or in Go's Menu, keeps, shares and exports routes, and opens files from other apps.

More: Save, share and export

Keep

  • Save route (signed in) puts the route in My routes, up to 200, each with a More button that opens its GPX, Rename, Versions (in Plan) and Delete. A search box finds a route by name. In Plan, once the route is up to date and you've picked it (when there is more than one line to choose from), the big button at the top of the map, where Update route sits, becomes Save route, with Start ride beside it (on a phone they read Save and Start). That Save also has Download GPX, and not signed in it offers Sign in with Discord (where available; your plan is kept) or Download GPX. A route someone sent you by link can be saved the same way. Any change to the plan brings Update route back. With nothing planned yet, that button is Load route, which opens a short list. Not signed in, Sign in with Discord comes first (where available). Then Open GPX file asks the ride type and whether to Snap to roads (streets and paths bikes may ride; the default) or keep it Exactly as drawn (even where bikes may not ride), then Choose file. Below are your saved routes, then the routes kept on this device; one tap opens one. A search box appears once there are more than six.
  • Keep for offline keeps a planned route on this device, up to 10, so it opens without signal, in the installed app or a browser tab. The last ride is kept too.
  • Before a route is exported or kept, RouteMaker warns if it goes through a closure.

Share

  • Copy link and Share give a link to the route. Share route: others can look it over, change it, or ride it their own way. Share ride: everyone rides this exact route, never re-routed, and each can choose to show where they are (see ride links).
  • Keep shared link: 1 year after last opened, Permanently, or Until a date. Shared links are listed in Save and load, where you can turn one off.
  • Share as image draws the route as a picture: Square 1080 × 1080, Portrait 1080 × 1350, Story 1080 × 1920 or Landscape 1200 × 630, with a QR code if you want one.
  • A shared link shows a preview in text messages, Discord and other chat apps: the route's line on a map of the streets, parks and water around it, with its distance and climb beside the RouteMaker icon. Home's circle is left blank.

Export

  • GPX track[3]; FIT course and TCX course, with turn cues[4]; PDF cue sheet and PDF handlebar strip to print; and a cue sheet image for a phone's lock screen.
  • Choose your bike computer (Any, Garmin Edge, Wahoo ELEMNT or Hammerhead Karoo) so the cues and the course fit what it can hold.
  • Send to Garmin sends the course to Garmin Connect. With Garmin connected in Settings it adds the course to Garmin Connect directly, under the route's saved name (or its file name when it is not saved), and sending a saved route again updates that course instead of adding a copy; your Garmin picks it up at its next sync. Without a connection the course goes as a FIT file, as before, and so it does if Garmin turns the course down or cannot be reached: the line under the button gives the reason first.
  • Send to Wahoo appears once you are signed in and Wahoo is connected in Settings. It adds the route to your Wahoo app as a FIT course with turn cues, fitted to a Wahoo ELEMNT, and the app sends it to your ELEMNT. Sending a saved route again updates it. If Wahoo turns it down, cannot be reached or is no longer connected, the FIT course is saved instead, for the Wahoo app to open, and the line under the button gives the reason first.

Open a file

  • Open GPX… reads a file in your browser; it is never uploaded. Up to 20 MB and 100,000 points. A file's points without a name, and the points added to fit a track, become via points. Named points become stops and keep their names; a name that only says what the point is, such as Stop 2 or Start, is dropped, and a point named Via point, as RouteMaker writes them, stays a via point.
  • In Plan, Snap to the map's streets turns the track into a plan you can change, or Follow the file exactly rides it as drawn, for a road closed for an event. Load route's Snap to roads and Exactly as drawn are the same choice, made before the file opens.

Settings and your account

Settings holds My Go, the riding and display settings, the app install, and your account. Sign in to keep your settings and routes on every device.

More: Settings and your account
  • My Go: Home (once set, one line with Change) and Go's ride type. Kept with your account when you are signed in, otherwise on this device. The app opens in Go on a phone and in Plan on a computer, a shared route included; a tab keeps the view it was last in, and pressing Go or Plan counts for that tab only.
  • Riding and Display: cues, large text, rider and bike weight, high contrast and the rest, on Accessibility.
  • Install RouteMaker adds it to your home screen when the browser offers it. When a new version is ready, a line says so with Reload; it never interrupts a ride.
  • Account: Sign in with Discord keeps My Go, starred ride types and saved routes with your account. Delete account says what is deleted and what is kept, and asks once.
  • Account > AI assistants lists the assistant apps you connected, each with Disconnect, and links to AI assistants for how to connect one.
  • Garmin (signed in, where offered): Connect Garmin takes you to Garmin's page to allow it and brings you back to Settings, which says whether it worked. RouteMaker only sends the courses you choose and reads nothing from Garmin but your account number. It keeps Garmin's sign-in token, encrypted, that number, which course each saved route was sent as, and when you connected and last sent one. Disconnect asks first, and Send to Garmin then goes back to the file. More: the privacy page.
  • Wahoo (signed in, where offered): Connect Wahoo works as Connect Garmin does, through Wahoo's page. RouteMaker only sends the routes you choose and reads nothing from Wahoo but your account number. It keeps Wahoo's sign-in token, encrypted, that number, which routes it sent, and when you connected and last sent one. Disconnect asks first, and the routes already sent stay in your Wahoo app. Deleting your account also removes from Wahoo up to 100 routes sent through your current connection; any it cannot reach stay in the Wahoo app.

Reports and ride links

Reports holds your 311 checklist, for problems on the street you report to the city, and the ways to suggest a correction to the map's admins. Ride links let a group see each other on the route.

More: Reports and ride links

Suggest a change

  • Press and hold a road (or right-click it), then Suggest a change…: a road's traffic stress, a junction, or a hazard on a Mass Ride. The road at the map's center in Reports does the same without the map.
  • Nothing changes until an admin reviews it. Admins work through suggestions, closures and hazards in Review, beside Go and Plan.

311

  • During a ride, the 311 button shows all six problems at once: Pothole, Downed tree, Vehicle parked in a bike lane, Roadway or bikeway obstructed, Streetlight out and Something else. One tap logs it where you are, with no note to type. Where RouteMaker can send DC reports itself, a DC problem is sent at once and goes straight to Archived. In Plan, the map's right-click or long-press menu has Log for 311.
  • The log is a checklist in Reports, grouped by the 311 website for each place (DC 311[5], Montgomery County, Prince George's County, Baltimore and others). Report each one there, then check it off. Each entry says when it was logged, and once checked off, when it was reported. The log is kept with your account.
  • An entry left unchecked for 30 days moves to Archived by itself, marked not reported. It is kept there, and checking it off there still records it as reported.
  • When you end a ride with problems still to report, the ride summary says how many and that Reports is in Go's Menu and Plan's bottom bar, with an Open Reports button.

Group Ride and Mass Ride links

  • Broadcast, a small button beside Start ride in Plan and Go where your browser can share, opens your phone's share sheet with a link to the route. Once you send it, your ride starts and you are its leader. If the route isn't ready or the share fails, it says so beside the button and nothing starts. Share ride makes you the leader too; sharing the same route again, without leaving the page, keeps you the leader of both links.
  • Everyone who opens the same link is on the same ride, and sees the others as dots, with no names.
  • Opening a Share ride or Broadcast link follows the leader: the map moves with the leader's dot, which has a wide ring round it. You see it at once, before you answer whether to show yourself. A link opened in Plan follows the leader too, so someone at home can watch the ride.
  • While the leader rides with the app open, their phone sends the route it is riding (dashed, so a re-route shows) and the path it has taken so far (solid), about every 2 minutes and whenever the route changes, so someone who opens the link late sees where the ride has been. The start of the path is left out until it is 650 ft [200 m] from where it began, as rides often start at home.
  • Moving or zooming the map stops following. Tap the riders count and turn on Follow the leader to follow again. While you ride yourself, the map follows you instead.
  • When the link opens, it asks once whether to show me as a dot, and your role: Participant, Lead line, Sweep or Corker. Your dot is sent only while the app is open, and only within a quarter mile [400 m] of the route. On a Share ride or Broadcast link, while you ride, it is shown on or off the route, so the leader and riders who get separated can find each other. It stops when you turn it off (tap the riders count), close the app, are off the route for 10 minutes (not counted while you ride a Share ride or Broadcast link), or 12 hours after the start.
  • Alert sends one tap to everyone on the link with your location. A Group Ride has Rider off the back; Crash; Mechanical or flat, stopping; Wrong turn; Lost, separated from the group; Wait up, regroup; and Other. A Mass Ride has Extensive rider spread; Emergency vehicle coming through; Aggressive person; Medical emergency or crash; Rerouting, road closed; Lost, separated from the ride; and Other. Crash and Medical ask first and offer to call 911.
  • In Alert, Other, on the website where your browser supports it (it does not work in the Android app), Speak fills the box from what you say; check it, then tap Send. It needs a connection: your browser's maker (such as Google for Chrome or Apple for Safari) turns the recording into text.
  • The server keeps dots, the leader's route and path, and alerts in memory only, for 2 minutes. A phone's random ride code and its alert counts are forgotten 2 minutes after it last checked in.
  • Planning a Mass Ride: Mass Ride.

The Android app

The Android app is RouteMaker with cues that keep going when the screen is locked[6].

More: The Android app
  • On first launch it asks for the site's address.
  • RouteMaker links shared to the app open in it.
  • Send to Garmin hands the course to the Garmin Connect app, or adds it to Garmin Connect directly once Garmin is connected. Connecting works in the app from its next update; until then, connect on the website and the connection works in the app too.
  • Send to Wahoo works the same way once Wahoo is connected; connecting it in the app works from the app's next update.
  • Locked-screen cues, the ride notification and the buzzes: Accessibility.
